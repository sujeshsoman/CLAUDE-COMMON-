# Go-to-market strategy and pricing benchmarks: online MRI console simulator for radiographers (India, Kerala first; Gulf/UK diaspora second), as of Nov 2026

_Method note for the report writer: in this session, page fetching (WebFetch) was blocked by the network policy for every external host tried (corsmed.com, itnonline.com, razorpay.com). All findings below come from web-search result summaries with the URL of the underlying page. Prices that come from third-party, competitor-marketing or aggregator pages are flagged. USD-to-INR conversions use an assumed rate of about ₹88 per US$. That rate is my estimate and not sourced. Treat every converted figure as approximate._

## 1. Pricing benchmarks: what Indian learners pay for comparable ed-tech and MRI training, and how global simulator vendors price

### Takeaway
Indian radiographers pay much less than doctors do. Kerala fresher radiographers earn about ₹15,000–25,000 a month. Mass-market online courses (Udemy, Entri) sell for ₹300–1,500. In-person MRI certificate courses cost ₹12,000 (ACTREC short course) up to ₹21,500–1,00,000 (private 6–12 month certificates). Doctor-facing NEET-PG apps cost ₹7,000–25,000 a year, which is a ceiling and not a comparable. The main global MRI simulator, Corsmed, launched a permanent free tier on 2 Nov 2026. Its paid plans run 3, 6 or 12 months with one account per user, and group pricing is quote-only.

### Inferences
- Willingness to pay of Indian radiography students and freshers is anchored near Udemy and Entri levels (₹500–1,500 one-time or yearly). Paying ₹12,000 or more happens only for credentialed, in-person or career-gate products (ACTREC, private certificates, DHA prep). A self-serve simulator sold to individuals in Kerala would likely need to sit at about ₹199–499 a month or ₹1,499–3,999 a year. A mentor-led cohort with a certificate could carry ₹4,999–12,000, below the ACTREC in-person fee. These are estimates from the benchmarks above and are not tested.
- Doctor-facing apps (Marrow/PrepLadder at ₹7k–25k a year) show that Indian learners pay premium annual prices when the product is tied to a high-stakes exam or job outcome. The closest radiographer equivalents are DHA/Prometric licensing and UK HCPC/NHS jobs, so diaspora-bound users are the segment most likely to pay premium prices.
- For Gulf/UK diaspora pricing, a DHA exam fee of about US$240 (about ₹21,000) and UK Band 6 MRI pay of about £40k+ suggest that a ₹4,999–9,999 a year (or about AED 199–399 / about £49–99) "exam + MRI console readiness" plan is defensible. This is an estimate.
- Corsmed's free tier (Nov 2026) resets the floor: a basic head/knee scanning demo is now free worldwide. A paid Indian product has to offer something Corsmed's free tier does not, such as Malayalam/Indian-context teaching, protocol packs matching scanners common in Indian hospitals, mentorship, exam alignment (DHA/HCPC/Kerala PSC), and India pricing.

### Gaps
- No official 2026 rupee price lists for Marrow, PrepLadder, DocTutorials, Unacademy medical or Coursera India were retrieved. All figures are third-party.
- No Kerala-specific short MRI-protocol course (in-person, e.g., Kochi/Bengaluru diagnostic chains) with a published 2026 fee was found.
- Corsmed's exact individual and institutional prices, and any student discount, could not be confirmed because the page could not be fetched.
- No published price was found for UK-built radiography e-learning or simulators (e.g., Shaderware, Virtual Medical Coaching), for Siemens/GE/Philips application training, or for ScanLabMR/ImagingU bundles.

## 2. Business models that work in Indian ed-tech for niche professional skills

### Takeaway
The best-documented Indian pattern is freemium funnelled from free social/YouTube content into low-priced yearly subscriptions (Entri, Xylem). Doctor-exam apps add premium yearly tiers and trials (PrepLadder). Typical freemium conversion is 2–5% (8–12% is "great"). Entri's reported paid ratio is about 4%. Global simulator vendors monetise through fixed-term individual licences plus quote-based institutional seats (Corsmed), so a B2B college/hospital licence is the natural second leg. EMI matters only at higher price points.

### Cited Findings
- Entri: free content plus a paid subscription. The founder said that "if you take Kerala alone as a market, we would be breaking even" (2020). ARR was US$2M in 2020. — [YourStory 2020](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/funding-alert-local-language-learning-app-entri-good-capital-1); [ETV Bharat](https://etvbharat.com/english/state/kerala/kerala-start-up-entri-eyeing-5-million-users-in-12-months/na20200204133806453)
- Xylem Learning (Kerala) runs free classes for more than 3 million students across 30 YouTube channels and reports more than 1,00,000 paid online students plus 30,000 in offline/hybrid centres. Physics Wallah agreed to invest about ₹500 crore for a 50% stake over 3 years (June 2023). — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2023/06/physics-wallah-pw-invests-rs-500-cr-consolidate-south-indian-market-xylem-learning); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/physicswallah-acquires-50-stake-in-xylem-in-a-61-mn-deal-to-chart-south-india)

- Affordability remains a central concern for ed-tech founders in Budget 2026 commentary, and taxation (18% GST) adds to learner cost. — [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/?p=93050)

### Inferences
- A tiered model fits the evidence best: (a) a free web demo (one anatomy, a few sequences), (b) a low-priced individual subscription with UPI (monthly and annual, annual discounted to pull cash forward), (c) a paid cohort (simulator + live mentor + certificate of completion) at a higher price, optionally on EMI if priced at ₹10k or more, and (d) B2B per-seat yearly licences for KUHS-affiliated colleges, private paramedical institutes and hospital training departments. B2B also limits individual price sensitivity and piracy.
- Expect about 2–5% of free demo users to convert to paid. Entri's roughly 4% paying ratio is a reasonable Kerala reference. A plan that needs 20% conversion is unrealistic.
- Certificates of completion are valued in Indian allied-health hiring, as job posts list certificates and registrations (Section 3), but a simulator certificate is not a regulated qualification. Position it as "CPD/skills evidence", not as a licence. This is an inference, and I found no source on how employers value it.
- White-labelling for colleges ("powered by Mallu Radiographer") is a plausible B2B add-on, but I found no Indian allied-health evidence of it.

### Gaps
- No Indian allied-health-specific conversion, churn or ARPU data were found. All conversion benchmarks are global SaaS.
- No evidence was found on what Kerala colleges or hospitals currently pay for any training software per seat.
- No independent 2025–2026 survey of Indian learners' price sensitivity by payment method was found. Most sources are vendor-commissioned.

## 3. Channels and partnerships (Kerala, India, Gulf, UK)

### Takeaway
The clearest partnership routes are: ISRT (Indian Society of Radiographers and Technologists) and its active Kerala chapter conference (SCISRT); the Kerala Paramedical Council (registration body); KUHS and LBS-allotted colleges; Kerala government overseas recruiters (ODEPC, NORKA Roots), which already run UK NHS radiographer drives; Kerala DHA/Prometric coaching institutes; and, in the UK, the Society of Radiographers' PAIR special interest group (Indian-origin radiographers), which started as a WhatsApp group and now runs an annual conference. Malayalam-first education has proven scale in Kerala (Entri, Xylem). I could not verify specific Malayalam radiography YouTube or Instagram competitors.

### Inferences
- Priority channel order for Kerala: (1) Mallu Radiographer's existing Instagram/YouTube with Malayalam reels showing "console challenges" that link to the free demo; (2) WhatsApp/Telegram broadcast groups for student cohorts (the PAIR WhatsApp origin and the KREST Telegram forwarding show this is how radiographers organise); (3) a stall or workshop at SCISRT/ISRT Kerala and KUHS college CMEs; (4) direct B2B pilots with 3–5 private paramedical colleges, where the 20-seat cohorts make a "class pack" a natural unit; (5) referral deals with DHA coaching institutes and ODEPC/NORKA pre-departure training.

## 4. Payment and platform logistics (gateways, UPI AutoPay, GST, LMS embedding, anti-sharing)

### Takeaway
Razorpay is the default Indian gateway: about 2% + GST for domestic payments including UPI, and 3% for premium methods and international cards. UPI AutoPay mandates can go up to ₹1,00,000, and recurring debits up to ₹15,000 need no extra authentication, which comfortably covers a simulator subscription. Stripe India is invite-only, and individuals cannot accept international payments. Online training is taxed at 18% GST, with a ₹20 lakh registration threshold (inter-state rules are disputed across sources, so check with a CA). Exports with an LUT are zero-rated. Indian creator LMSs take 10% (Graphy, TagMango free tier), and Teachable and Thinkific both allow iframe or custom-code embedding of an HTTPS web app.

### Cited Findings
**Gateways and fees**
- Razorpay India: 2% + 18% GST (about 2.36% effective) for domestic cards, UPI, netbanking and wallets. Premium methods (EMI, corporate cards, Amex/Diners, Pay Later) and international cards are 3% + GST. UPI carries zero MDR at the bank level, but Razorpay charges a platform fee. — [Razorpay pricing explained](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-payment-gateway-pricing-explained/); [Razorpay cheapest gateway blog](https://razorpay.com/blog/cheapest-payment-gateway-for-businesses-in-india/)
- An August 2026 third-party comparison confirms a flat 2% + GST for domestic payments and up to 3% for international cards. — [Xflow](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/payment-gateway-charges-comparison)
- Policy risk: Parliament passed an enabling amendment (2026) for possible future UPI MDR. The government says UPI remains free for users and small merchants. — [Razorpay blog](https://razorpay.com/blog/cheapest-payment-gateway-for-businesses-in-india/)
- Stripe India is invite-only. Invitees need an Import/Export Code and completed KYC, and individuals cannot accept international payments. News dates this to Oct 2025, and one guide says May 2024 (conflict). — [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/accepting-international-payments-from-stripe-accounts-in-india); [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/stripe-moves-to-invite-only-in-india)
- Alternatives for Indian sellers selling globally are listed (e.g., Dodo Payments' 2026 roundup). Paddle's changelog shows UPI AutoPay support added in 2026. Neither was verified in depth. — [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/razorpay-alternatives/); [Paddle changelog](https://developer.paddle.com/changelog/2026/upi-autopay/)

**UPI AutoPay / e-mandates**
- Razorpay Subscriptions: UPI AutoPay mandates up to ₹1,00,000 at mandate creation. — [Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/subscriptions/supported-payment-methods.md)
- Recurring debits up to ₹15,000 need no extra authentication (no PIN/AFA) under the RBI framework. Above that, extra authentication applies. — [PhonePe merchant help](https://cms.phonepe.com/en/mx/merchant-help/loans/managing-e-nach/why-do-i-need-to-accept-5000-or-15000-limit-for-setting-up-a-upi); [EximPe](https://eximpe.com/blog/saas/upi-autopay-for-saas-setup-limits-and-what-global-founders-need-to-know)
- The RBI Digital Payments E-mandate Framework, 2026 (circular dated 21 April 2026) removed the need for a fresh OTP for each recurring payment within defined limits. — [Paytm blog](https://paytm.com/blog/payments/upi/upi-mandates-with-paytm/)
- NPCI gave apps until 15 December 2026 to standardise EMI-on-UPI support. — [Cashfree](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/what-indias-emi-habit-means-for-global-checkouts/)

**GST and invoicing**
- Commercial online training and coaching is taxed at 18% GST. The registration threshold is ₹20 lakh aggregate turnover (₹10 lakh for special-category states; Kerala is not one). — [Disytax](https://disytax.com/?p=24958); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=964180)
- Conflict: one creator guide says any inter-state sale triggers mandatory registration regardless of turnover. A Udemy community post makes a similar claim for inter-state and export sales. This needs verification against primary law (CGST/IGST notifications). — [Disytax creators guide](https://disytax.com/?p=25009); [Udemy community](https://community.udemy.com/t5/First-time-course-creation/reg-GST-paypal-LUT/m-p/92655)
- Export of services (e.g., to Gulf/UK learners) is zero-rated if an LUT is filed before the first export invoice and payment is received in convertible foreign exchange. — [Disytax](https://disytax.com/?p=24958); [CA Sahuja](https://www.casahuja.com/2024/01/gst-on-online-education-and-export-of.html)

**LMS and creator platforms**
- Graphy (India) takes a 10% + GST revenue share per transaction (10% or ₹10, whichever is higher) plus a one-time ₹19,999 onboarding fee. — [Graphy support](https://graphy804657060717855613.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/1060000131262-how-does-the-revenue-share-pricing-model-work-on-graphy-)
- Graphy US plans: Launch US$49/mo, Rise US$149, Scale US$399, each with 0% transaction fee (third party). — [Cartmango](https://cartmango.com/graphy-pricing/)
- TagMango starts free with no monthly fee and a 10% commission including gateway fees, per a review. Paid plans at ₹5,000 and ₹15,000 a month reduce the fee to 5% and 2.5% (third party; another source claims 0.5–2%, a conflict). — [TagMango](https://tagmango.com/graphy-alternative); [Superprofile](https://superprofile.bio/blog/tagmango-alternatives); [Business News This Week](https://businessnewsthisweek.com/technology/best-platforms-like-graphy-for-online-communities/)
- TagMango FY25 revenue was ₹20.9 Cr (up 162.9% from ₹8.0 Cr) with ₹7.3 Cr PAT, which shows the scale of India's creator-course economy. — [Inc42 TagMango financials](https://inc42.com/company/tagmango/financials/)
- Exly uses commission-based pricing with lower commission for upfront plans and custom enterprise pricing. No rupee figures were found. — [Exly blog](https://exlyapp.com/blog/?p=5400)
- Teachable lessons support a "Custom Code" block for embed code, though Teachable support won't troubleshoot custom code. — [Teachable help](https://intercom.help/teachableknowledgebase/en/articles/11682457-embed-forms-and-other-content-into-lessons)
- Thinkific embeds external web content via iframe (new builder: "Interactive or Web content"). It must be HTTPS, and embeds don't report completion back to Thinkific. — [Thinkific embedded content](https://support.thinkific.com/hc/en-us/articles/39446156116759); [Thinkific embed troubleshooting](https://support.thinkific.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030742693)

**Anti-sharing and anti-piracy precedents**
- Marrow's "No Sharing" fair-usage policy: one user per account, a fixed 1,000 fair-usage hours for videos, and accounts blocked on violation. — [Marrow fair usage](https://www.marrow.com/fair_usage)
- Corsmed: one account per user, no shared accounts. — [Corsmed pricing](https://corsmed.com/pricing)
- Device binding (registering the first device and requiring removal before a new login) is a standard technique. — [VdoCipher glossary](https://www.vdocipher.com/glossary/device-restriction/); [Prep terms](https://prepedu.com/vi/terms-and-conditions-of-transactions)

### Inferences
- For a ₹199–499 monthly or ₹1,499–3,999 annual plan, use Razorpay Subscriptions with UPI AutoPay: the price sits under the ₹15,000 no-extra-authentication limit and suits a market where credit-card penetration is 6–7%. Offer annual plans as one-time UPI payments as well, since that is simpler and avoids mandate failures.
- Platform choice: a 10% take rate (Graphy/TagMango) on top of about 2.36% gateway cost is significant at low price points. Because the simulator is a custom web app, hosting the app yourself with Razorpay plus a simple LMS (or Teachable/Thinkific embedding the app via iframe) keeps control of login and anti-sharing. In that case the app must permit framing and track progress itself. This is an inference, and the trade-offs need to be tested.
- Anti-sharing for a web simulator: one active session per account (kick older sessions), device limit (e.g., 2), a per-user watermark on screens, and fair-usage scan quotas modelled on Marrow's hours cap. These are suggestions based on precedents and have not been tested in this market.
- For Gulf/UK sales, Stripe is likely unavailable for a small Indian business. Razorpay international (3% + GST) or a merchant-of-record service avoids this, and an LUT must be filed before the first export invoice.

### Gaps
- Cashfree and Instamojo pricing, Spayee, and Exly's rupee pricing were not retrieved.
- Whether Graphy, Exly, TagMango or Spayee allow embedding an external web app inside a lesson was not confirmed (only Teachable/Thinkific evidence was found).
- The exact GST position for small inter-state online service suppliers below ₹20 lakh, and for sellers using an e-commerce operator, remains unresolved. A CA should confirm. My understanding (not sourced here) is that service providers making inter-state supplies are exempt from compulsory registration below the threshold.
- No data was found on UPI AutoPay mandate failure or churn rates for Indian ed-tech.

## 5. Launch tactics with evidence

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced tactics are: free content at scale on YouTube and social channels feeding a paid product (Xylem: 3M free to 1 lakh paid; Entri: freemium at ₹300–1,500 a year); short full-access trials (PrepLadder 24 hours); ungated free tiers (Corsmed now does this for MRI); and community-led webinars and conferences (PAIR). I found no rigorous Indian evidence on ambassador programmes or early-bird pricing.

### Cited Findings
- Xylem: 30 YouTube channels serving more than 3M free students, converting to more than 1 lakh paid online students plus 30k offline. It was later valued via PW's ₹500 Cr for 50%. — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2023/06/physics-wallah-pw-invests-rs-500-cr-consolidate-south-indian-market-xylem-learning)
- Entri: Malayalam-first freemium, more than 1 crore registered and 4 lakh paying. It reported 10x revenue growth over two years (2022 claim). — [YourStory 2022](https://yourstory.com/2022/09/kochi-online-learning-startup-empowering-aspirants-vernacular-language)
- PrepLadder: 24-hour full premium trial on app download. — [PrepLadder](https://www.prepladder.com/neet-pg-study-material/preparation-strategy/free-vs-premium-features-on-prepladder)
- Ungated freemium (no signup required to try) converts at a "good" 7–9% in ChartMogul's data. — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)
- Free introductory modules may lift online course conversion (opinion, not measured). — [Single Grain](https://www.singlegrain.com/blog/ms/free-to-paid-conversion-rates/)
- Corsmed's own lead-gen move: free forever head/knee simulator (Nov 2026). — [Corsmed free simulator](https://www.corsmed.com/free-mri-simulator)

### Inferences
- A launch sequence consistent with the evidence:
  1. A free browser demo with no login for the first scan, then email/WhatsApp capture to save results.
  2. Weekly Malayalam "fix this scan" reels and quizzes (e.g., artifact spotting, TR/TE choices) linking to the demo.
  3. A founding-member annual price for the first cohort, communicated through WhatsApp/Telegram broadcast groups.
  4. Live cohort #1 (simulator + weekly mentor call + completion certificate) to create testimonials.
  5. Workshops at college CMEs and SCISRT in exchange for class-pack pilots.
  6. A PAIR/UK webinar and DHA-coaching referral partnerships for the diaspora tier.
- Timing: launch the free demo before LBS allotments (Aug–Sep) and first-year starts, and push B2B pilots to colleges during academic planning. Exact KUHS exam calendars were not retrieved.
- The demo must clearly beat or differ from Corsmed's free head/knee tier: Malayalam narration, Indian vendor-style console layouts, and protocol challenges tied to common Indian referrals (e.g., spine, brain stroke).

### Gaps
- No Indian evidence was found on campus-ambassador programme conversion, early-bird discount effectiveness, or quiz/challenge virality for allied-health content.
- No named Indian allied-health or radiography creator case study (revenue or conversion) on Exly, TagMango or Graphy was found.

## 6. Risks: willingness to pay, piracy, undercutting, seasonality

### Takeaway
The biggest risks are: (1) low individual willingness to pay, with fresher pay at ₹15–25k a month and Udemy anchoring at ₹449–599; (2) a free global competitor, since Corsmed's free tier launched 2 Nov 2026 and an open-source eduMRIsim exists; (3) account sharing, which both Marrow and Corsmed explicitly police; (4) price undercutting, which is already common in Indian medical ed-tech; (5) seasonality around Kerala's May–Sept admissions cycle; and (6) regulatory and payment changes (possible UPI MDR, Stripe invite-only, GST ambiguity).

### Cited Findings
- Fresher radiographer pay in Kerala is mostly ₹15,000–25,000 a month, and some X-ray tech posts pay ₹8,000–15,000. — [Evanios Mavelikara](https://www.evaniosjobs.com/job-apply/2481752/xray-technician-job-in-mavelikara); [PERSOL](https://www.persolindia.com/articles/current-salary-of-mri-technician-in-india)
- Udemy imaging courses discounted to ₹449–599. — [Careers360](https://www.careers360.com/college/31278/certificate/354048)
- Corsmed free tier with no time limit (Nov 2026). — [ITN](https://www.itnonline.com/node/91310)
- eduMRIsim, an open-source MRI simulator. — [TU/e](https://boost.tue.nl/projects/educational-magnetic-resonance-imaging-simulator-edumrisim)
- Undercutting precedent: Oncourse markets itself at ₹6,999 a year against Marrow and PrepLadder. — [getoncourse.ai](https://getoncourse.ai/blog/prepladder-vs-marrow-neet-pg-2026-comparison)
- Account-sharing controls: Marrow blocks accounts that violate one-user and hours caps, and Corsmed bars shared accounts. — [Marrow fair usage](https://www.marrow.com/fair_usage); [Corsmed](https://corsmed.com/pricing)
- Seasonality: LBS paramedical applications open around May–June, with allotments from September (nursing; dates partly conflicting). — [keralaevents](https://news.keralaevents.in/?p=215); [FindMyCollege](https://articles.findmycollege.com/news/lbs-bsc-nursing-trial-allotment-2026-out-check-results-register-options-by-sep-9-at-lbscentre-in/)
- Payment and regulatory risk: a possible future UPI MDR for larger merchants, Stripe India invite-only, and GST at 18% adding to learner cost. — [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/cheapest-payment-gateway-for-businesses-in-india/); [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/accepting-international-payments-from-stripe-accounts-in-india); [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/?p=93050)
- No-refund policies are standard in Indian ed-tech (Marrow, DHA Prometric fees), but they raise the bar for trust in a new brand. — [Marrow Pro](https://www.marrow.com:443/pro); [Leap Scholar](https://leapscholar.com/blog/?p=84953)

### Inferences
- Mitigations:
  - Shift revenue weight to B2B per-seat licences (colleges and hospitals), which lowers individual price sensitivity and sharing.
  - Keep the individual tier cheap, monthly-cancellable and UPI-first.
  - Differentiate on Malayalam, Indian context and mentorship rather than on simulator breadth versus Corsmed.
  - Enforce single-session logins and watermarking.
  - Time campaigns to admission and first-year intake windows, with an exam-revision push before KUHS/DHA exam periods.
- Diaspora segments (DHA/Prometric and UK NHS recruits) have higher stakes and higher income, so they can subsidise low Kerala student pricing. Their purchase triggers are licensing exams and job onboarding, which are not tied to the Kerala academic calendar and may smooth seasonality.
- A 7–14 day full-access trial or a refund window for the first month would help counter trust barriers for a new brand. This is an inference, given that the no-refund norm comes from established brands.

### Gaps
- No data was found on piracy or credential-sharing rates in Indian allied-health ed-tech.
- KUHS university exam calendars for allied-health programmes were not retrieved, so exam-period seasonality is unconfirmed.
- No evidence was found of any Indian company currently selling an MRI console simulator, so the competitive threat inside India is unknown. Searches found only foreign or academic simulators.
