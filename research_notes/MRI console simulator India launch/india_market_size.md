# India (and Kerala) market for MRI technologist / radiographer training: size and demand drivers (as of Nov 2026)

Research note compiled 2026-11-08. Method caveat for the report writer: this session's network blocked direct fetches of several key primary pages (lbscentre.in, dme.kerala.gov.in, theprint.in, battleias.com, currentaffairspulse.com). Figures attributed to those URLs come from search-engine extracts of those pages, not from reading the full documents, and should be checked against the originals before they go into a business plan. Most market-size numbers below are secondary or estimated. Each one is labelled OFFICIAL, INDUSTRY ESTIMATE, AGGREGATOR or SELF-REPORTED.

## 1. MRI installed base: India and Kerala (scanners, density, growth, 1.5T vs 3T, centres)

### Takeaway
India has roughly 2,500 to 4,800 MRI scanners. The 4,800 figure is a 2023 government estimate. Independent and industry estimates run 2,500 to 3,500. That works out to about 1.5 to 3.5 scanners per million people, against roughly 38 per million in the US. Additions run at about 250 to 400 scanners a year, and the government expects annual demand to more than double by 2030. By value, 1.5T systems hold a slight majority of the market, and 3T is the faster-growing segment. I found no official count for Kerala, only facility-level evidence.

### Cited Findings
**Installed base and density (India)**
- OFFICIAL (government estimate, July 2023): "the total installed base of MRI is 4800." The same release puts annual demand at under 350 machines and expects it to more than double by 2030. Source: [PIB, Ministry of Science & Technology release on indigenous 1.5T MRI, PRID 1944717](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1944717&reg=48&lang=2)
- INDUSTRY ESTIMATE: 2,500 to 3,000 MRI scanners, concentrated in metros and large private chains. More than 90% of installed MRI machines are imported, and the article projects 200 to 300 new installations a year over the next decade. Source: [Healthcare India, "India's Indigenous MRI Moment"](https://healthcare-in-india.net/healthcare-delivery/uncategorized/indias-indigenous-mri-helium-free-medical-devices-market/) (also at [archive 9277](https://healthcare-in-india.net/archives/9277))
- INDUSTRY ESTIMATE: about 3,500 MRI machines "including low field, 1.5T, 3T and refurbished machines," with 350 to 400 installed a year. Source: [Indian Radiologist, "Made in India MRI"](https://www.indianradiologist.com/index.php/review/made-in-india-mri). I took the attribution from a search extract and have not verified it in the full text.
- INDUSTRY ESTIMATE (trade press, 2021): about 2,800 installed. The 2021 Indian MRI equipment market was ₹1,786 crore and 291 units, and private hospitals and diagnostic centres account for about 230 new scanners a year. Sources: [Medical Buyer, "MRI market bounces back"](https://medicalbuyer.co.in/mri-market-bounces-back/); [Medical Buyer, "Government, the savior of MRI market"](https://medicalbuyer.co.in/government-the-savior-of-mri-market-2/). The search extract merged these two articles, so I could not confirm which article gives which figure.
- COMPANY ESTIMATE (2024): India has "fewer than 5,000" MRI scanners, about 3.5 per million, against about 38 per million in the US. Source: [Hyperfine press release via BusinessWire, May 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240528157535/en)
- OLDER / PROJECT ESTIMATE: 1.5 to 2 MRI machines per million in India, against 10 to 30 in developed countries. Source: [IUAC, Indigenous MRI (IMRI) project page](https://www.iuac.res.in/en/imri)
- MARKET REPORT: "3.5 units per every million" population (no data year given). Source: [Insights10, India MRI Market Report](https://www.insights10.com/report/india-mri-market-analysis/)
- WHO tracks MRI units per million as an indicator, but the search did not surface an India value. Source: [WHO GHO indicator](https://www.who.int/data/gho/data/indicators/indicator-details/GHO/total-density-per-million-population-magnetic-resonance-imaging)

**Growth and field-strength mix**
- MARKET REPORT: 1.5T systems held 55.72% of India's MRI market in 2025. Very-high-field (≥3T) systems are forecast to grow at a 7.72% CAGR over 2026 to 2031. Source: [Mordor Intelligence, India MRI Market](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-magnetic-resonance-imaging-mri-market)
- MARKET REPORT: India's MRI market is forecast to grow at a 4.9% CAGR, led by tertiary-care networks buying higher-field systems. Source: [Future Market Insights, Global MRI Market 2026–2036](https://www.futuremarketinsights.com/reports/magnetic-resonance-imaging-mri-market)
- TRADE PRESS (2021): 3T led by value at 52.5% against 47.2% for 1.5T, with 3T concentrated in metros and Tier-I cities. In 2019, 1.5T had the largest volume share at 52%. Sources: [Medical Buyer](https://medicalbuyer.co.in/mri-market-bounces-back/); [Medical Buyer](https://medicalbuyer.co.in/government-the-savior-of-mri-market-2/)
- OFFICIAL (Competition Commission of India market study): a 1.5T unit costs about ₹6 to 8 crore and a 3T about ₹12 to 14 crore. Source: [CCI, Market Study of Diagnostic Medical Imaging Equipment Industry in India](https://www.cci.gov.in/public/images/marketstudie/en/market-study-of-diagnostic-medical-imaging-equipment-industry-in-india1724145632.pdf)
- Indigenous MRI: in March 2025, AIIMS New Delhi said it would install India's first indigenously built 1.5T MRI (IMRI mission, SAMEER as implementing agency) by October 2025 for trials. Source: [Business Standard, Mar 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/health/aiims-delhi-to-install-india-s-first-indigenously-built-mri-machine-by-oct-125032501270_1.html). I found no confirmation that the installation was completed.

**Kerala (facility-level only)**
- Metro Scans & Laboratories runs 15 centres across Kerala. Four of them (Trivandrum, Kollam, Kottarakkara, Karunagappally) have 1.5T MRI. The company also claims the "first and only 3T MRI facility as a standalone diagnostic center in South Kerala" at Pioneer Metro Scans, Trivandrum. Source: [Metro Scans, About](https://metroscans.com/page/About-our-Diagnostic-Centre)
- Amrita Hospital, Kochi lists 1.5T and 3.0T MRI scanners plus a 3.0T PET-MR. Source: [Amrita Hospitals Kochi, Radiology](https://www.amritahospitals.org/kochi/department/speciality-departments/radiology)
- Under an agreement with the state, HLL Lifecare (Hindlabs) set up MRI centres at the Thrissur, Alappuzha and Kottayam government medical college hospitals, as a public-private partnership. Sources: [Hindlabs Thrissur](https://hindlabs.in/hll-lifecare-opens-hindlabs-mri-scan-centre-thrissur-medical-college); [Hindlabs Kottayam](https://hindlabs.in/hll-has-set-its-third-hindlabs-mri-scan-centre-govt-medical-college-kottayam). These pages are undated or old.

**Radiologist context (a proxy for demand on imaging departments)**
- About 20,000 to 22,000 practising radiologists in India, roughly 1 per 100,000 people. Sources: [5C Network](https://www.5cnetwork.com/resources/radiologist-shortage-india); [Medical Buyer, "one radiologist per lakh"](https://medicalbuyer.co.in/india-has-alarming-ratio-of-one-radiologist-per-lakh-people/)
- OFFICIAL (NMC seat matrix): 3,224 MD Radio-diagnosis seats for 2026-27, including 471 in Karnataka and 400 in Maharashtra. Source: [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/education/3224-md-radiodiagnosis-radiology-seats-as-per-nmc-seat-matrix-2026-27-471-in-karnataka-400-maharashtra-180747)

### Inferences
- **Business-plan range for India's installed base:** use about 3,000 as a low case and about 4,800 as a high case (the government figure). The government number probably includes low-field and refurbished units. At about 300 to 400 additions a year, the late-2026 base is likely around 3,500 to 5,500 if the 2023 government figure is the right anchor. This is an extrapolation, not a sourced figure.
- **Kerala scanners (my estimate, not sourced):** Kerala has roughly 2.5 to 2.8% of India's population. That share comes from Census 2011 general knowledge (about 3.34 crore people), which I have not re-sourced here. Kerala's private-hospital density is higher than average, so a range of about 120 to 200 MRI scanners in Kerala is a reasonable placeholder until a state figure is found. A single chain (Metro Scans) operates at least five MRI sites, which is consistent with a three-digit state total.
- **Field-strength mix for the simulator:** 1.5T is still the volume workhorse, and 3T is growing faster. A simulator should default to 1.5T protocols and offer 3T-specific content (SAR, B1 inhomogeneity, chemical-shift differences) as a differentiator or premium module.
- **Implied MRI-technologist workforce (my estimate):** scanners usually run two shifts or more and need about 2 to 4 technologists each. At 3,000 to 5,000 scanners, that implies about 6,000 to 20,000 people working on MRI consoles in India today, and about 250 to 800 in Kerala. Treat this as an assumption-driven estimate. Every 300 to 400 new scanners a year creates roughly 600 to 1,600 new MRI-console jobs, plus replacement demand from attrition and Gulf migration.

### Gaps
- No official, current (2025 to 2026) national MRI census was found. AERB does not license MRI, so no AERB registry covers it, and none of the search results gave a figure from the Ministry of Health or the National Health Profile.
- No Kerala-specific count of MRI scanners or MRI centres was found, and no breakdown of government versus private ownership. The Kerala Health Department, KASP/PM-JAY empanelment lists and IRIA Kerala chapter are the likely sources.
- The exact unit split of 1.5T versus 3T (as opposed to share by value) was not found for 2025 to 2026.

## 2. Radiography / medical imaging technology programmes: colleges, seats, graduates, MRI console exposure

### Takeaway
I found no national count of radiography or imaging-technology colleges or seats. Individual programmes typically admit 15 to 60 students a year. NCAHP is now standardising qualification names and curricula, including a two-year competency-based D.MRIT notified in 2026 and four-year UG degrees. In Kerala, LBS Centre allots the Government-quota seats in B.Sc paramedical degree courses, including Medical Radiological Technology, across government and self-financing colleges. DME's paramedical diploma seat matrix totals 867 seats across all diploma courses, but I could not isolate the radiology share. International evidence consistently shows that students get little hands-on MRI console time during placements, and that simulation improves their confidence.

### Cited Findings
**National programme landscape**
- The NCAHP Act 2021 recognises "Medical Radiology, Imaging and Therapeutic Technology Professional" as a category covering radiology and imaging technologists, nuclear medicine technologists and radiotherapy technologists. Source: [NCAHP Act 2021, India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/16824/1/aA2021-14.pdf)
- NCAHP-notified undergraduate programmes follow a standard four-year structure. Source: [NIPS Future Skills, NCAHP allied healthcare education 2026](https://nipsfutureskills.com/ncahp-allied-healthcare-education-india/)
- NCAHP notified a competency-based two-year Diploma in Medical Radiology and Imaging Technology (D.MRIT) with a six-month internship, under "One Nation One Curriculum," reported in October 2026. Source: [BattleIAS current affairs, 7 Oct 2026](https://battleias.com/current-affairs/2026-10-07-ncahp-notifies-autonomous-boards-and-digital-registration-for-allied-and-healthc/) (secondary). The second sentence is also reported by [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/regulations-to-strengthen-quality-assessment-transparency-in-allied-and-healthcare-professions-notified/3064754/). Neither page could be fetched in full.
- A 2012 PHFI / Ministry of Health (National Initiative for Allied Health Sciences) study identified 139 allied health programmes and found "no clear data" on how many trained allied workers exist. Sources: [Down To Earth, "Short on paramedics"](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/short-on-paramedics--43129); [NIAHS report (SlideShare copy)](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/niahs-report/73062526). This is dated.
- Typical intakes (AGGREGATOR): Karnataka B.Sc imaging and radiology programmes admit about 15 to 30 seats each, for example BMCRI Bengaluru 20 and KMC Manipal 20. Source: [CollegesInfo, Radiology colleges Karnataka](https://collegesinfo.org/blogs/radiology-imaging-technology-colleges-karnataka)
- Amrita's Bachelor of Medical Radiology & Imaging Technology (Faridabad listing) has 20 seats. Source: [Amrita University B.MRIT](https://www.amrita.edu/program/bachelor-medical-radiology-imaging-technology/)
- Shiksha shows aggregated seat counts for B.Sc radiology categories (for example "2171+"), but its labels do not make clear what the numbers cover, so they are not reliable as a national total. Source: [Shiksha, B.Sc Radiology and Imaging Technology](https://www.shiksha.com/medicine-health-sciences/bsc-radiology-and-imaging-technology-chp)

**Kerala**
- OFFICIAL: The Director of LBS Centre runs centralised allotment of "Government Seats" in B.Sc nursing and paramedical degree courses under KUHS. These cover government colleges and the Government-quota seats in self-financing colleges; management seats are filled by the colleges. The 2026 prospectus is issued under G.O.(Rt)No.1964/2026/H&FWD dated 08-06-2026. - OFFICIAL: Medical Radiological Technology is listed among the B.Sc paramedical courses in the LBS centralised process. Source: [LBS Centre 2023 paramedical degree prospectus](https://lbscentre.in/paradegreealltmnt2023/downloads/prospectus.pdf)
- OFFICIAL: The DME Kerala seat matrix lists a "Para Medical Diploma Courses" table totalling 867 seats across the government medical colleges at Thiruvananthapuram, Alappuzha, Kottayam, Ernakulam, Thrissur, Kozhikode and Kannur, plus a separate entry of 30 seats at the Government Institute for Paramedical Sciences, Kannur. The extract does not give course-wise numbers, so the DRT (radiological technology) share is unknown. Source: [DME Kerala Seat Matrix](https://dme.kerala.gov.in/seat-matrix/). Fetch was blocked, so this comes from a search extract.
- Private colleges offering B.Sc MRT in Kerala include AIMS (Amrita) Kochi, Sree Gokulam and Somervell Memorial CSI. Source: [Campushunt blog, B.Sc Radiology colleges in Kerala](https://campushunt.in/blog/bsc-radiology-colleges-in-kerala/). Fees are in section 6.

**Hands-on MRI console exposure**
- In a phenomenological study of undergraduate MRI placements (Malta), students described a lack of hands-on practice and little supervision. The authors linked this to curriculum gaps, inadequate placement resources and the absence of advanced training opportunities. Source: [Radiography (Elsevier), "Radiography students' clinical placement experiences in MRI"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1078817414000960)
- A qualitative systematic review of radiography placements found that a lack of hands-on CT and MRI practice is a recurring concern. Rising student-to-supervisor ratios limit practice opportunities. Source: [ResearchGate, systematic review of radiography students' placement experiences](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342201279_Radiography_Students'_Perceptions_and_Experiences_of_their_Clinical_Placements-_A_Qualitative_Systematic_Review)
- MRI-simulated training sessions were well received by undergraduate radiography students and improved their confidence before hospital practice. Source: [J. Medical Imaging and Radiation Sciences, "Transforming MRI Education through Simulation-Based Training"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1939865416301114)
- A 2025 mixed-methods study compared 80 newly qualified radiographers (40 trained with immersive VR, 40 with traditional simulation) on clinical preparedness. Source: [PMC12449592](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12449592/). Related work compares VR and physical simulation in first-year students in South America: [PMC10258633](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10258633/)
- An Indian programme example: SIHS Pune's B.MRIT combines classroom teaching with hands-on clinical training and lists MRI among its training areas. This is a programme description, not outcome data. Source: [SIHS B.MRIT](https://www.sihs.edu.in/bachelor-medical-radiology-imaging-technology)

### Inferences
- **Seat estimate approach (my estimate):** with no national figure, a bottom-up model is needed. If India has about 400 to 800 institutions offering a radiography or imaging degree or diploma, at an average intake of about 20 to 40, that is roughly 8,000 to 32,000 entrants a year across all levels. This is an assumption, not data, and should be replaced by an NCAHP institution-registry count once NCAHP publishes one.
- **Kerala (my estimate):** if radiological technology is one of about 8 to 12 paramedical diploma courses in the DME matrix, DRT may be about 70 to 120 of the 867 government diploma seats. B.Sc MRT across government and self-financing colleges is plausibly another 100 to 300 seats a year. This needs verification from the LBS seat matrix PDF, which was blocked here.
- **Product implication:** MRI is expensive (₹6 to 14 crore per scanner, from the CCI study above) and kept busy for revenue, and supervisors have many students each. So students' console time is structurally scarce. A web-based console simulator speaks directly to the "lack of hands-on MRI practice" theme in the literature. NCAHP's competency-based D.MRIT and UG curricula give institutions a hook ("competencies in imaging procedures and equipment") for buying simulation.

### Gaps
- No national count of colleges or annual seats for B.Sc MRIT, RIT or MRT, DRT, DMRIT or M.Sc MRIT was found. NCAHP has not yet published an institutional registry, or none surfaced. State health universities (KUHS, RGUHS, MUHS, Dr NTRUHS, TN Dr MGR) would need to be counted college by college.
- No course-wise Kerala seat matrix (DRT, B.Sc MRT, M.Sc MRT) could be read. The lbscentre.in and dme.kerala.gov.in fetches were blocked and those hosts need to be allowed.
- No India-specific study quantifying students' MRI console hours was found.
- No M.Sc MRIT seat data was found.

## 3. Workforce: number of radiographers, shortage, salaries, valued MRI skills

### Takeaway
I found no credible current headcount of radiographers or imaging technologists in India. NCAHP registration, which opened in 2026, should eventually produce one. The documented shortages are old and concentrated in rural public facilities: a 40% radiographer shortfall at Community Health Centres, and a 2012 PHFI estimate of a 6.5 million gap across all allied health professions. Self-reported MRI technologist pay is low in India and Kerala, around ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 a month, against roughly USD 4,000 to 5,000 a month in the Gulf. That gap is the main economic driver for upskilling and migration.

### Cited Findings
**Headcount and shortage**
- A health ministry report cited by Deccan Herald shows a 40% shortage of radiographers at rural Community Health Centres. Source: [Deccan Herald, "An emergency: A strained paramedical network"](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fan-emergency-a-strained-paramedical-network-3289516)
- A shortage of 3,629 radiographers at CHC level. Source: [Healthcare Workforce in India (Sabu), PDF](https://rfums-bigtree.s3.amazonaws.com/files/resources/healthcareworkforceindia-sabu-8.pdf). The attribution comes from a search extract, and the figure likely derives from an older edition of Rural Health Statistics.
- PHFI and GE (2012) reported a supply-demand gap of 6.5 million allied health professionals in India. Source: [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/short-on-paramedics--43129). This is dated, and it covers all allied health professions, not radiographers alone.
- A PHFI and WHO report puts the ratio of allied health workers to doctors at 1:1. Source: [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fan-emergency-a-strained-paramedical-network-3289516). The attribution comes from a search extract.
- Professional bodies publish no member counts. The Society of Indian Radiographers (SIR) offers life membership only to Indian radiological technologists, at Rs 1,500 for residents. Source: [SIR Life Membership](https://radiographers.org/SIR-Lifemembership/). Source: [ResearchGate, "The shortage of radiographers: A global crisis in healthcare"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374851430_The_shortage_of_radiographers_A_global_crisis_in_healthcare)

**MRI skills employers pay for**
- The DHA (Dubai) MRI technologist licence is a separate category from general radiographer. Source: [MyDoctorLicense, DHA License for MRI Technologist](https://mydoctorlicense.com/dha-license-for-resonance-magnetic-imaging-mri-technologist/) (consultancy site)

### Inferences
- Indian pay of roughly ₹3 to 6 lakh a year against Gulf pay of roughly ₹40 to 50 lakh a year (USD 4,000 to 5,000 a month at about ₹83 to 85 per USD, which is my own conversion) is a 7 to 10x gap. That makes MRI specialisation and documented MRI experience a high-value credential for Kerala technologists aiming at the Gulf.
- Self-reported salary data for Kerala is too thin (2 to 3 data points per city) to rely on. A business plan should cite ranges and label them as indicative.
- Employers appear to pay a premium for demonstrated MRI-specific experience, which is what gates the DHA MRI licence, rather than for general radiography. A simulator cannot substitute for the 2 years of documented clinical experience. It can, however, be positioned as protocol and console fluency preparation that shortens on-the-job ramp-up and supports licensing exam preparation.

### Gaps
- No national or Kerala headcount of radiographers or imaging technologists was found. NCAHP's registry, once populated, should provide it. Kerala's state council for allied health professions was not found in these searches.
- No current (2024 to 2026) Rural Health Statistics radiographer sanctioned, in-position and shortfall figures were retrieved.
- No job-portal counts of MRI technologist openings in India or Kerala (Naukri, Indeed) were retrieved.
- No evidence was found on specific MRI sub-skills that attract pay premiums in India (for example cardiac MRI, 3T, MR spectroscopy, paediatric sedation workflows).

## 4. Regulation: NCAHP Act 2021 status, registration, curriculum, simulation and CPD

### Takeaway
NCAHP became operational in 2026. Registration Regulations were reportedly gazetted on 31 August 2026: online registration, five-year validity, about Rs 2,000 to register and Rs 1,000 to renew, with renewal tied to CPD. Secondary sources say the CPD requirement is about 15 hours a year (75 over five years), with at most 50% online. That would create a recurring CPD market that an online simulator could serve if NCAHP approves online CPD providers. NCAHP has also notified autonomous boards, digital registration and a competency-based D.MRIT curriculum. I found no explicit simulation or skills-lab mandate.

### Cited Findings
- The NCAHP Act 2021 is the statute, and it covers the "Medical Radiology, Imaging and Therapeutic Technology Professional" category. Source: [India Code, NCAHP Act 2021](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/16824/1/aA2021-14.pdf)
- The Registration of Allied and Healthcare Professionals Regulations, 2026 were reportedly gazetted on 31 August 2026 and took effect the same day. Registration is valid for five years, and fees are reported as Rs 2,000 for registration and Rs 1,000 for renewal. Source: [CurrentAffairsPulse, 31 Aug 2026](https://currentaffairspulse.com/article/2026-08-31-ncahp-registration-allied-healthcare-professionals-regulations-2026) (secondary; fetch blocked)
- Renewal depends on meeting Continuing Professional Development (CPD) requirements through approved online and offline modes. Secondary sources report at least 15 CPD hours a year (75 over five years), with online CPD capped at 50%. A lapsed registration can move to "inactive" status. Sources: [ClarityUPSC, NCAHP new registration rules](https://clarityupsc.com/news/article/ncahp-new-registration-rules-for-allied-healthcare-professionals-4264); [CurrentAffairsPulse](https://currentaffairspulse.com/article/2026-08-31-ncahp-registration-allied-healthcare-professionals-regulations-2026). These are UNVERIFIED against the Gazette text, and the search extracts did not cleanly say which source gives which detail.
- NCAHP notified autonomous boards and digital registration, along with a competency-based two-year D.MRIT with a six-month internship. The curriculum covers the "knowledge, skills, values and attitudes required for safe, effective and ethical practice in medical radiology and imaging services." The internship is meant to develop "practical skills, professional competencies, patient-care practices, radiation safety practices and familiarity with imaging procedures and equipment under appropriate supervision." Sources: [BattleIAS, 7 Oct 2026](https://battleias.com/current-affairs/2026-10-07-ncahp-notifies-autonomous-boards-and-digital-registration-for-allied-and-healthc/); [ThePrint, "Regulations to strengthen quality, assessment, transparency in allied and healthcare professions notified"](https://theprint.in/india/regulations-to-strengthen-quality-assessment-transparency-in-allied-and-healthcare-professions-notified/3064754/). Both are secondary and neither could be fetched.
- Registration runs on the NCAHP online portal (ncahp.abdm.gov.in). Required documents include Aadhaar, PAN, photo, school and degree certificates and internship proof, and provisional registration may apply during internship. Degrees must match NCAHP-notified names and curricula. Source: [Virohan, How to complete NCAHP registration in 2026](https://www.virohan.com/blog/how-to-complete-ncahp-registration-in-2026) (education-company blog)
- The Medical Radiology, Imaging and Therapeutic Technology category is reported to include MRI, CT, mammography and sonography sub-roles. Source: [ePsychology, What is NCAHP Registration](https://www.epsychology.in/blog/what-is-ncahp-registration/) (secondary)
- One career site notes that entry requirements for the technologist path were "still being settled" in 2026. Source: [Career Plan B, Radiology as a career in 2026](https://www.careerplanb.co/radiologist-vs-radiology-technologist/)

### Inferences
- **CPD market sizing (my estimate):** if every registered imaging technologist needs about 15 CPD hours a year and up to half can be online, each registrant represents about 7.5 online CPD hours a year. With 20,000 to 60,000 registered radiology and imaging technologists nationally (a placeholder range; no headcount was found), that is about 150,000 to 450,000 online CPD hours a year. This is entirely conditional on the CPD figures being confirmed and on NCAHP accrediting online providers.
- Name and curriculum standardisation (D.MRIT, B.MRIT) will push institutions to align with NCAHP competencies over the next 1 to 3 years. That is a procurement window for skills-lab and simulation tools, even without an explicit mandate.
- Because MRI involves no ionising radiation, it falls outside AERB's licensing remit (per the brief; I found no source to cite). So NCAHP is the only regulatory lever for MRI-specific competency.

### Gaps
- The official Gazette texts of the NCAHP Registration Regulations 2026, the minimum standards for institutions (lab, skills-lab or simulation requirements) and the CPD provider-accreditation rules were not read. The ncahp.abdm.gov.in and egazette.gov.in sources need to be checked.
- No explicit simulation or skills-lab requirement for radiology or imaging programmes was found.
- The status of Kerala's State Allied and Healthcare Council under the Act was not found.
- No information was found on whether existing practitioners trained under older diplomas (DRT, DMRIT) are grandfathered.

## 5. Migration demand: Gulf, UK, Ireland, Australia/NZ; whether licensing favours MRI experience; exam-prep markets

### Takeaway
Gulf licensing (DHA, DOH, SCFHS, QCHP and others) is the main outbound route for Kerala radiographers. It typically requires about 2 years of post-qualification experience and a Prometric computer-based exam booked through DataFlow verification, with MRI as a tested domain at roughly 10%. A separate DHA MRI-technologist licence requires 2 years of MRI-specific experience. A sizeable consultancy and question-bank prep market already exists. In the UK, more than a quarter of HCPC radiographer registrants trained overseas, and the NHS runs a Global Radiographers Programme, but I found no India-specific numbers. Kerala's emigrant stock is about 2.2 million (2023), though the survey data does not break out radiographers.

### Cited Findings
**Gulf licensing and prep market**
- DHA radiographer licensing requires a bachelor's in radiography (or equivalent diploma) and 2 years of post-qualification experience. The exam is booked through Prometric, and a fresh graduate may be exempt from the experience requirement in some cases (anecdotal). Reported formats conflict: 100 MCQs in 150 minutes with a 55% pass mark, against 150 MCQs over 3 hours. Total cost is estimated at Rs 50,000 to 80,000 across application, Prometric and licence fees. Sources: [LeapScholar, DHA Exam for Radiographer 2026](https://leapscholar.com/blog/dha-exam-for-radiographer-guide/); [MyDoctorLicense, DHA license for radiographer](https://mydoctorlicense.com/dha-license-for-radiographer/); [PrometricMCQ, DHA exam guide](https://www.prometricmcq.com/general-guide-dha-exam-way-apply-prometric-exam-uae/). All are consultancy or prep sites, so the details are unverified.
- MRI is one of the tested domains, at about 10% of content according to one source. Another describes 11 core domains including MRI. Source: [Ziapy, Gulf radiography licensing exams: DHA, SCFHS & QCHP](https://ziapy.com/gulf-radiography-licensing-exams-dha-scfhs-qchp/)
- A separate DHA MRI-technologist licence exists. It requires a bachelor's or diploma in MRI (or radiography plus a postgraduate MRI diploma) and 2 years of MRI experience. Source: [MyDoctorLicense, DHA MRI Technologist](https://mydoctorlicense.com/dha-license-for-resonance-magnetic-imaging-mri-technologist/)
- DataFlow primary-source verification is part of the DHA radiographer process. Source: [Overseas Healthcare Consultant, DHA DataFlow for Radiographer](https://www.overseashealthcareconsultant.com/dha-dataflow-for-radiographer/)
- Paid exam-prep products for Gulf radiography licensing already exist, such as DHA radiology question banks. Sources: [DHAexam.com, DHA Radiology Exam Questions](https://dhaexam.com/dha-radiology-exam-question/); [PrometricMCQ](https://www.prometricmcq.com/general-guide-dha-exam-way-apply-prometric-exam-uae/); [DHA License consultancy](https://ar.dhalicense.ae/dha-license-for-radiographers/)
- Job demand indicators (AGGREGATOR): 324 radiographer vacancies listed in the UAE [Jobsora UAE](https://ae.jobsora.com/jobs-radiographer); Middle East radiologic technologist listings as of Oct 2026 [Bayt.com](https://www.bayt.com/en/international/jobs/radiologic-technologist-jobs/); MRI radiographer postings in Abu Dhabi [PlacementIndia, MRI Radiographer jobs UAE](https://www.placementindia.com/international/mri-radiographer-jobs-in-uae.htm)
- Kerala government recruitment channels (ODEPC, NORKA) mainly recruit nurses; in 2023 ODEPC was recruiting nursing staff for Saudi Arabia, Belgium and Germany. Source: [Onmanorama, Jul 2023](https://www.onmanorama.com/career-and-campus/top-news/2023/07/25/avoid-intermediaries-for-overseas-nursing-opportunities-try-kerala-govt-odepc-norka.html). I found no current ODEPC or NORKA radiographer drive.

**UK (HCPC / NHS)**
- A 2024 national survey reports that overseas-trained radiographers are over a quarter of all HCPC radiographer registrants (12,160 of 46,071). In its small sample of migrant radiographers, 2 of 8 respondents came from India. Source: [Radiography (2024), "Mapping the migrant diagnostic radiographers in the UK"](https://www.radiographyonline.com/article/S1078-8174(24)00215-3/pdf)
- NHS England's Global Radiographers Programme is described as the only in-house nationwide NHS programme for recruiting overseas diagnostic radiographers. Source: [HEE Global Radiographers Programme](https://www.hee.nhs.uk/sites/default/files/documents/Global%20Radiographers%20Programme%20v1.pdf). See also [Staffbank case study, NHS England International Diagnostic Radiography Recruitment Programme](https://staffbank.net/case-study/nhs-england-international-diagnostic-radiography-recruitment-programme/)
- Since November 2025, international HCPC applicants must map their qualifications, experience and training against the HCPC standards. Source: [NHS Employers, Recruitment of overseas AHPs](https://www.nhsemployers.org/articles/recruitment-overseas-allied-health-professionals)
- By mid-2022, the HCPC's median time to a first decision on international applications was over 90 weeks, according to the PSA. Source: [Wikipedia, HCPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_and_Care_Professions_Council). This is dated.

**US certification routes (ARRT / ARMRIT)**
- ARMRIT accepts foreign degrees with an official transcript evaluation. One pathway is cross-training from an allied health field with at least 1,000 hours of documented MRI clinical experience. ARMRIT says it has certified technologists in Asia and the Middle East and is recognised by the ACR MRI Accreditation Committee. Sources: [ARMRIT application (2026, Kryterion)](https://www.armrit.org/pdf/APPLY82026Kryterion.pdf); [ARMRIT application Aug 2025](https://armrit.org/pdf/APP08152025.pdf)
- The ARRT RT(MR) primary pathway requires a JRCERT-accredited programme and an associate degree, whereas ARMRIT does not. Source: [TeslaMR, ARRT vs ARMRIT 2026](https://teslamr.com/guides/arrt-vs-armrit-mri-certification/) (third-party guide)

**Kerala emigration context**
- The Kerala Migration Survey 2023 estimates about 2.2 million emigrants (against 2.1 million in 2018) and about 1.8 million returnees (against 1.2 million in 2018). Women make up 19.1% of emigrants, and student emigrants number about 250,000 (11.5%). Sources: [IIMAD, KMS 2023 Report](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf); [IASGyan summary](https://www.iasgyan.in/daily-current-affairs/international-institute-of-migration-and-development)
- One extract says the GCC share of Kerala emigrants fell from 93.8% (1998) to "19.5%" (2023). That is very likely a misreading: 19.5% is probably the non-GCC share, which would put the GCC share around 80.5%. Verify in the KMS report. Source: [IASGyan](https://www.iasgyan.in/daily-current-affairs/international-institute-of-migration-and-development)

### Inferences
- The Gulf route rewards documented MRI experience (the DHA MRI licence) and exam readiness (MRI is about 10% of the radiographer exam). A simulator can be sold as part of a "Gulf-ready MRI" bundle: console and protocol practice plus exam-style MRI MCQs. It complements the existing question-bank market rather than competing with it.
- The UK and Ireland routes depend on mapping competencies against HCPC and CORU standards. A simulator's logged competency records might support a candidate's portfolio narrative, but there is no evidence that regulators accept simulator hours.
- ARMRIT's 1,000-hour MRI clinical requirement and DHA's 2-year MRI experience rule both count real clinical exposure. Simulator time is unlikely to count toward them, so marketing should not claim that it does.

### Gaps
- No count of Indian or Kerala radiographers migrating each year, or of Gulf licences issued to Indian radiographers (DHA, DOH, SCFHS, QCHP, OMSB, Kuwait MOH, NHRA Bahrain), was found.
- No HCPC country-of-training breakdown for India was found. HCPC annual reports or an FOI request would be needed.
- No information on Ireland (CORU) or Australia/NZ (ASMIRT, MRPBA, MRTB) recognition of Indian radiography qualifications, or on any MRI experience preference, was retrieved.
- No current SCFHS (Saudi) radiography classification or exam details were retrieved beyond the Ziapy overview.
- No figures for Indian candidates taking ARRT or ARMRIT were found.

## 6. Ability and willingness to pay: course fees, short MRI courses, online prep spending

### Takeaway
Willingness to pay is modest but real. A KUHS government-seat B.Sc MRT costs about ₹1.3 to 1.65 lakh in total, and private Kerala colleges charge about ₹40,000 to ₹95,000 a year. Short MRI technician certificates cost about ₹20,000 to ₹80,000. Gulf licensing costs candidates about Rs 50,000 to 80,000. NCAHP registration is Rs 2,000 every five years. Monthly technologist pay of about ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 caps individual spending. Pricing in the low thousands of rupees a year for individuals, with institutional site licences, looks consistent with these anchors. I found no data on how much students spend on online prep.

### Inferences
**Price anchors (my inference):**
- An individual student subscription of about ₹1,500 to ₹5,000 a year is about 1 to 4% of annual B.Sc fees, and below a short MRI certificate (₹20,000 to ₹80,000).
- A Gulf-prep bundle of about ₹3,000 to ₹10,000 sits at about 5 to 15% of total DHA licensing cost.
- An institutional licence of about ₹50,000 to ₹2,00,000 a year per college is a tiny fraction of scanner capex, and comparable to one student's annual private-college fee.
