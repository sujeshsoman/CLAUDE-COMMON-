**Yes, launch it, but as a lean, brand-led product that competes on language, career pathways and college workflow rather than on having the best simulator.** Plain access to an MRI simulator stopped being scarce on **2 November 2026**. That day Corsmed, the Swedish market leader whose UK customers include UCLH and The Christie, opened a **free tier with no time limit** covering head and knee scanning **[UNVERIFIED]** ([ITN](https://www.itnonline.com/node/91310)). The "UK virtual MRI console" is most likely MRI Education's browser console, which is sold only inside an **£800 course** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([MRI Education](https://www.mrieducation.com/apply/)). Your most direct rival in India is not in Kerala but in Delhi: **ScanFlo** sells browser MRI and CT simulators at roughly **₹899–14,999** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([ScanFlo](https://scanflo.io/mri)). The research could not identify the Kerala launch you heard about. The closest match is **SIMLABS in Thrissur**, a VR clinical-simulation company launched in 2026 that lists radiology modules but no MRI console **[UNVERIFIED]** ([SIMLABS](https://simlab.in/)). The market is small. Kerala is worth roughly **₹1 crore a year or less** and India in the low tens of crores **[ESTIMATE]**, and fresher radiographers in Kerala earn about **₹15,000–25,000 a month** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Evanios](https://www.evaniosjobs.com/job-details/2539082/radiographer-job-in-kaloor)). Individual plans must therefore be priced near Udemy levels, and the margin must come from colleges and from radiographers heading to the Gulf or the UK. The legal risks are manageable on five conditions: build an original console that copies no vendor's screens, keep every claim educational, use only synthetic or commercially licensed images, file the trade mark in classes 9, 41 and 42, and get advice on a **Corsmed US patent covering slice selection in a web-based simulator** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12260951)). Technically, an engine that computes images in the student's browser keeps hosting to an estimated **US$50–150 a month** and an MVP to about **1,000–1,600 developer hours** **[ESTIMATE]**, and the vendor-neutral prototype being built in this repository is a sound starting point. The report is written for the owner of the Mallu Radiographer brand. The research links the brand to Sujesh Soman, a Kerala-born radiographer in Manchester, and anyone else launching under that name would collide with an active personal brand.

## Corsmed's free tier and ScanFlo's rupee pricing already set the floor

The UK product you heard about is almost certainly **MRI Education's "Virtual MRI Console"**. Dr John Talbot, who co-presents the *MRI in Practice* course with Dr Catherine Westbrook, built it in-house, and it runs in any browser with no install ([MRI Education](https://www.mrieducation.com/)). It is not sold on its own. It comes bundled with a CPD-endorsed physics and technique course at **£800 all-inclusive**, which includes 12 months of simulator access **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Apply page](https://www.mrieducation.com/apply/)). The home page says the price rises to £900 from January **[UNVERIFIED]** ([MRI Education](https://www.mrieducation.com/)). The Australasian price is A$1,600 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Australasia page](https://www.mrieducation.com/australasia/)). MRI Education markets to Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, Papua New Guinea and Europe, but has no India or Gulf page. The other candidate for the "UK simulator" is **Corsmed**. It is Swedish, but UK radiographers meet it through NHS and university case studies at UCLH, The Christie, Cumbria and Teesside ([HealthySimulation](https://www.healthysimulation.com/vendors/corsmed/)). The UK simulation firms Shaderware and RadSimTech make CT and X-ray products, not MRI ([HealthySimulation – Shaderware](https://www.healthysimulation.com/vendors/shaderware/); [RadSimTech](https://radsimtech.co.uk/about-us/)).

**Corsmed is the competitor that matters.** Its vendor-neutral browser simulator offers 25+ sequences and 30+ adjustable parameters **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Corsmed](https://www.corsmed.com/mri-simulator)). It computes images by solving the Bloch equations on GPU servers ([Corsmed](https://corsmed.com/about-mri-simulators/how-does-corsmeds-mri-simulator-work)), and it raised a **US$3.5M seed round** in December 2024 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2024/12/corsmed-raises-3-5m-in-funding.html)). The new free tier covers only head and knee with about **seven sequences**. ITN says "eight" but lists seven **[UNVERIFIED]** ([ITN](https://www.itnonline.com/node/91310)). The 60+ anatomies, pathology simulation and LMS integration stay behind the paywall **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Corsmed free](https://www.corsmed.com/free-mri-simulator)).

Corsmed's individual pricing is unclear. One page shows a **US$129, 12-month bundle** with a textbook and seven courses, yet its markup also shows "$175/month" **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Corsmed for individuals](https://www.corsmed.com/en-us/mri-simulator/for-individuals)). An undated news page lists €950 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Corsmed News](https://corsmed.com/news)). Institutional licences are quote-only, run for 3, 6 or 12 months, and are never sold by the hour **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Corsmed FAQ](https://www.corsmed.com/faqs/can-i-buy-timed-access-on-corsmeds-simulator-and-only-pay-per-hour-of-use)). The research found **no Corsmed partner in India or the Gulf**, only a South Korean distributor ([Corsmed](https://www.corsmed.com/news/corsmed-enters-the-south-korean-market-with-medical-standard)). Because the free tier needs no card, though, Indian students will find it anyway.

One detail from Corsmed's own UCLH case study shapes the rest of this report. New radiographers there reached competence in **3–4 months instead of 6**, but home study underperformed structured, supervised sessions on site **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Corsmed UCLH case study](https://www.corsmed.com/case-studies/nhs-university-college-london-hospitals)). Simulators sell best when they come wrapped in an educator's workflow.

**ScanFlo is the Indian product closest to yours.** Zeka Labs Private Limited of Rohini, Delhi, runs it; the company's registration number dates it to 2023. It offers browser simulators for MRI, CT, PET-CT and X-ray. Students run spin-echo, gradient-echo and inversion-recovery sequences, plan slices on localisers and adjust TR, TE, TI and flip angle live. Access starts with a free 45-minute trial, followed by "Student" and "Student Pro" plans priced in rupees **[UNVERIFIED]** ([ScanFlo](https://scanflo.io/); [ScanFlo MRI](https://scanflo.io/mri)). The site's structured data gives an MRI price range of **₹999–14,999** and a CT range of ₹899–13,999, without saying which plan costs what **[UNVERIFIED]** ([ScanFlo CT](https://scanflo.io/ct)). A Delhi/Noida reseller, 3D Paramedical, lists **US$39 and US$49 a month** as one-off payments with no auto-renewal, plus seat-based cohort licences **[UNVERIFIED]** ([3D Paramedical](https://3dparamedical.com/mri-scanflo)). No founder names, user numbers or reviews surfaced. You therefore cannot claim to be "India's first MRI console simulator", but you can claim to be the first one in Malayalam, built for Kerala's radiographers and the routes their careers take.

The rest of the field is vendor-locked, expensive, or free but rough. Siemens Healthineers India offers a cloud **SmartSimulator** that reproduces Siemens screens and is run with a Siemens application expert ([Siemens India](https://www.siemens-healthineers.com/en-in/services/customer-services/upskill-services/equipment-and-clinical-education/individual-education-and-practice/smartsimulator)). When educators rated TU Eindhoven's eduMRIsim, "clinical workflow resemblance" scored lowest, at **3.6 out of 5** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([ISMRM 2025](https://archive.ismrm.org/2025/1958.html)).

## Kerala is a sub-₹1-crore beachhead; India and the diaspora carry the business

India has **2,500–4,800 MRI scanners**, depending on the source. The upper figure is a 2023 government estimate, which also expects annual demand to more than double by 2030 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1944717&reg=48&lang=2)). Industry estimates run lower, at 2,500–3,500, with **250–400 new installations a year** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Healthcare India](https://healthcare-in-india.net/healthcare-delivery/uncategorized/indias-indigenous-mri-helium-free-medical-devices-market/); [Indian Radiologist](https://www.indianradiologist.com/index.php/review/made-in-india-mri)). That is about **3.5 scanners per million people against about 38 in the US**, which leaves a long runway for growth **[UNVERIFIED]** ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240528157535/en)). By value, 1.5T systems held **55.72%** of the market in 2025, while 3T is growing faster **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-magnetic-resonance-imaging-mri-market)).

Kerala has no official scanner count. The research team's placeholder estimate is **120–200 scanners** **[ESTIMATE]**, and facility-level evidence fits a three-digit total. Metro Scans runs 15 centres, at least four with 1.5T and one with 3T **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Metro Scans](https://metroscans.com/page/About-our-Diagnostic-Centre)). Amrita Kochi runs 1.5T, 3T and PET-MR scanners **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Amrita](https://www.amritahospitals.org/kochi/department/speciality-departments/radiology)). A **₹15 crore 1.5T scanner** at Parippally Government Medical College is due to open on 11 November 2026 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2026/11/03/advanced-mri-facility-installed-at-parippally-government-medical-college-hospital.html)). For the product, that argues for 1.5T protocols as the default, with 3T content as a premium module.

Nobody has counted the MRI workforce either. At an assumed 2–4 technologists per scanner, India has roughly **6,000–20,000 people working at MRI consoles** and Kerala roughly **250–800** **[ESTIMATE]**. Training cohorts are small. One KUHS-sanctioned BSc MIT course has **20 seats** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([MMC](https://academy.mmchospital.in/storage/cms_files/Sanctioned%20intake%20as%20on%2020.11.2024.pdf)). Kerala's DME paramedical diploma seat matrix totals **867 seats across all courses**, but the radiology share is unknown **[UNVERIFIED]** ([DME Kerala](https://dme.kerala.gov.in/seat-matrix/)).

The problem a simulator solves is well documented. Students repeatedly report too little hands-on MRI practice on placement, because student-to-supervisor ratios keep rising ([Radiography](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1078817414000960); [systematic review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342201279_Radiography_Students'_Perceptions_and_Experiences_of_their_Clinical_Placements-_A_Qualitative_Systematic_Review)). At ₹6–14 crore a scanner, departments cannot hand console time to trainees **[UNVERIFIED]** ([CCI](https://www.cci.gov.in/public/images/marketstudie/en/market-study-of-diagnostic-medical-imaging-equipment-industry-in-india1724145632.pdf)), and simulated sessions raise students' confidence before they reach the hospital ([JMIRS](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1939865416301114)).

Pay sets what individuals can spend, and it also drives migration. Fresher radiographer jobs in Kerala mostly pay ₹15,000–25,000 a month, and dedicated CT/MRI posts pay ₹30,000–50,000 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Evanios](https://www.evaniosjobs.com/job-details/2539082/radiographer-job-in-kaloor); [Meitra listing](https://www.tryapplynow.com/jobs/meitra-hospital-calicut-ct-mri-technologist-2d354624-9caa-46ae-be55-1f46ac6a7dcd)). Gulf MRI technologists earn about **US$4,000–5,000 a month**, a 7–10x gap **[UNVERIFIED]** ([IHR Canada](https://ihrcanada.com/benefits/radiographers-in-saudi-arabia-uae-and-qatar-benefits-package-and-salary-scale-guideline/)). One prep provider puts MRI at about 10% of Gulf radiographer licensing-exam content **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Ziapy](https://ziapy.com/gulf-radiography-licensing-exams-dha-scfhs-qchp/)). Dubai's separate MRI-technologist licence requires **two years of MRI-specific experience** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([MyDoctorLicense](https://mydoctorlicense.com/dha-license-for-resonance-magnetic-imaging-mri-technologist/)). In the UK, **12,160 of 46,071 HCPC radiographer registrants trained overseas** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Radiography 2024](https://www.radiographyonline.com/article/S1078-8174%2824%2900215-3/pdf)). The North West's CAMRIN programme interviewed in India and turned **110 offers into 63 recruits** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Cheshire & Merseyside NHS](https://www.cheshireandmerseyside.nhs.uk/media/pmob1pqx/a1-case-studies-recruitment.pdf)).

A new domestic driver may also be emerging. NCAHP registration regulations were reportedly gazetted on 31 August 2026, with five-yearly renewal tied to CPD. Secondary sources put the requirement at **15 hours a year, at most half of it online** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([CurrentAffairsPulse](https://currentaffairspulse.com/article/2026-08-31-ncahp-registration-allied-healthcare-professionals-regulations-2026); [ClarityUPSC](https://clarityupsc.com/news/article/ncahp-new-registration-rules-for-allied-healthcare-professionals-4264)). ## Original screens, educational claims and clean data keep legal risk low

**You may copy how an MRI console works, but not how a particular vendor's console looks.** In the EU, *SAS v World Programming* held that software functionality is not protected by copyright ([SCL](https://www.scl.org/2451-sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-ltd-report-and-analysis/)). In the US, *Lotus v Borland* treated a menu command hierarchy as an unprotectable "method of operation" ([Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/uncopyrightable-method-of-operation:-the-lotus-development-v.-borland-international-decision/view)). In India, section 52(1)(ac) of the Copyright Act permits observing and studying a program to understand its underlying ideas **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Copyright Amendment 1999](https://kwx.liehendr.com/content/wikisource_en_all_maxi_2022-09/A/Page%3AIndian_Copyright_Act_%285th_Amendment%29_1999.djvu/3)). The generic workflow (registration, localiser, planning, parameters, scan), textbook parameter names and protocol trees are therefore free to use. Expression is a different matter. A GUI can still be an ordinary copyright work in the EU and UK ([Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/graphical-user-interfaces-can-be-protected-by-copyright-rules-european-court)). The *SAS* litigation found infringement where a user manual's wording was copied ([8 New Square](https://8newsquare.co.uk/sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-ltd-2013-ewhc-69-ch/)). The Calcutta High Court held on **9 March 2026** that GUIs can be registered as designs in India **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1813770/graphical-user-interfaces-guis-qualify-for-design-registration-under-the-designs-act-calcutta-high-court)). In practice, design your own layout, icons and palette. Never trace screenshots or reuse manual text, and never market the product as "syngo-like". Mention vendors only in plain factual text, with a non-affiliation notice, because Indian courts read nominative use narrowly, as in *Toyota v Prius* ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/trademark/879442/doctrine-of-nominative-fair-use-under-trademark-law)).

**The patent is the risk most founders would miss.** US 12,260,951 B2, "Method for simulation of a magnetic resonance scanner", is assigned to Corsmed AB. It was granted on 25 March 2025 with priority from June 2020. Its first claim covers importing a pulse-sequence calculation model, setting input data and performing **slice selection in a web interface of a web-based MRI simulator** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([USPTO Gazette](https://patentsgazette.uspto.gov/week12/OG/html/1532-4/US12260951-20250325.html); [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12260951)). A related patent, US 12,326,484 B2, also surfaced, but its owner was not confirmed **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12326484)). A US patent binds only in the US, and no one checked for Indian or European equivalents. An engine that computes analytic equations on the student's device works differently from Corsmed's server-side Bloch engine, but only a patent attorney can judge whether the claims still reach it. Get a freedom-to-operate opinion before any US sales, and before relying on web slice planning in any market where an equivalent patent turns up.

**A training simulator stays outside medical-device law as long as it is never marketed as clinical.** CDSCO's guidance on medical-device software excludes software for education or training without a medical purpose; the guidance was issued in draft in October 2025 and reportedly finalised on 21 July 2026 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([CDSCO](https://www.cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/Guidance-document-on-Medical-Device-Software-under-MDR-2017.pdf); [Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/from-code-to-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance-15623)). The EU's MDCG 2019-11 and the UK MHRA likewise decide status by the manufacturer's stated purpose **[UNVERIFIED]** ([OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2019-11); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/medical-devices-software-applications-apps)), and a disclaimer cannot rescue a product whose marketing implies clinical use ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/mhra-issues-updated-guidance-on-ehealth-stand-alone-software-including-apps)). Keep "diagnose", "use on your patients" and "upload patient DICOM" out of the product and your Instagram posts. Avoid features that analyse real studies or connect to PACS, and label SAR values as illustrative.

**Data licences decide which images you may sell.** fastMRI and OASIS are non-commercial, so both are ruled out ([fastMRI](https://fastmri.med.nyu.edu/); [OASIS](https://sites.wustl.edu/oasisbrains/files/2024/04/DataUseAgreement_July2022-b14b9acfd16bd1b3.pdf)). IXI is CC BY-SA 3.0, so images derived from it must carry the same licence **[UNVERIFIED]** ([TorchIO](https://docs.torchio.org/_modules/torchio/datasets/ixi.html)). OpenNeuro releases new datasets as CC0, but each dataset needs checking **[UNVERIFIED]** ([OpenNeuro](https://openneuro.readthedocs.io/faq.html)). TCIA data are mostly CC BY, but brain and head-and-neck collections moved to restricted access in January 2022, because faces can be reconstructed from them **[UNVERIFIED]** ([TCIA](https://wiki.cancerimagingarchive.net/x/NIIiAQ)). Images from your own workplace are the riskiest source, because patient privacy, employer ownership, ethics approval and facial re-identification all bear on them. That is as true of NHS images as Indian ones; only properly anonymised data falls outside UK confidentiality guidance ([RCR](https://www.rcr.ac.uk/media/slmbkf0d/rcr-publications_guidance-on-maintaining-patient-confidentiality-when-using-radiology-department-information-systems-second-edition_november-2019.pdf)). The prototype's procedural phantom sidesteps all of this.

**Student data, the brand and consumer law each need a few hours of setup.** India's DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025. Most duties begin around **13–14 May 2027**: notice and consent, security safeguards, a 72-hour breach report, and verifiable parental consent with no tracking for anyone under 18 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1713500/regulatory-update-notification-of-the-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025); [AMLegals](https://amlegals.com/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/)). Selling to UK users brings in the UK GDPR and possibly a UK-representative duty ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/section/27)). File "Mallu Radiographer" in classes 9, 41 and 42 at **₹4,500 per class** by e-filing as an individual or start-up **[UNVERIFIED]** ([IP India](https://www.ipindia.gov.in/pages/trade-marks/learn/forms-and-official-fees)). Expect a section 9 descriptiveness objection, because "Radiographer" describes the service **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/section-9-11-grounds-refusal-trademark-india)); a logo filed with prior-use evidence improves the odds. Once the mark is advertised, third parties have four months to oppose it **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/trademark/1604054/understanding-trademark-opposition-in-india-what-every-brand-owner-needs-to-know)). Separately from the law, "Mallu" reads as slang in some contexts, so test how it lands with UK and Gulf audiences. Selling from your own site makes you an e-commerce entity. You need a published grievance officer who acknowledges complaints within 48 hours and resolves them within a month **[UNVERIFIED]** ([SS Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020/)). Dark patterns are now enforced: in June 2026 the CCPA fined PhysicsWallah **₹5 lakh**, partly for demanding data before users could open "free" content **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/1799382/dark-patterns-in-ed-tech-ccpa-imposes-penalty-on-physicswallah)). Call your certificate a "certificate of completion" until someone endorses it. College of Radiographers CPD Now endorsement costs a for-profit provider **£278.50 + VAT** for two years and would give you a genuine claim for UK buyers **[UNVERIFIED]** ([CoR](https://collegeofradiographers.ac.uk/education/education-approval/programme-approval/fees)).

## An in-browser analytic engine keeps the build modest and hosting near zero

Of the four ways to generate images, only one suits a low-cost console for a mass market. Full Bloch simulation (KomaMRI, JEMRIS and Corsmed's coreMRI lineage) gives the most realism. But someone pays for the GPUs: coreMRI runs on AWS GPU instances and needed eight GPUs to cut hour-long simulations to minutes **[UNVERIFIED]** ([KomaMRI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02702); [coreMRI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6524794/)). Pre-computed image libraries grow too large once every parameter combination is multiplied out. AI contrast synthesis is an offline tool for building tissue maps, not a real-time engine ([SyMTIC](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04173v1)). That leaves analytic computation in the browser, which the Heidelberg–Mannheim SimMRI/VirtMRI project proves out: the server only delivers static files, which its authors call "well suited for large student groups" ([ISMRM 2023](https://archive.ismrm.org/2023/2396.html)).

The recommended hybrid engine works in three steps. First, it computes each voxel from tissue maps with textbook equations: `S = PD·(1−e^(−TR/T1))·e^(−TE/T2)` for spin echo and FSE, `S = PD·|1−2e^(−TI/T1)+e^(−TR/T1)|·e^(−TE/T2)` for inversion recovery, FLAIR and STIR, and the spoiled-GRE Ernst form with T2* decay. Second, it moves the image into k-space and applies the acquisition there: wrap from a small FOV, chemical shift, motion ghosting, RF spikes, Gibbs ringing, partial Fourier, FSE echo-train blurring and parallel-imaging noise, as TorchIO does for ghosting and spikes **[UNVERIFIED]** ([TorchIO](https://docs.torchio.org/transforms/augmentation/RandomGhosting)). Third, it adds noise scaled by voxel volume, averages, bandwidth and coil. A small Bloch mode can still serve physics explainers, with optional "high-fidelity" scans queued to a KomaMRI server, as MRSeqStudio does ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00011v1)). A vendor-neutral prototype on this architecture is being built in this repository at `mri-console/index.html`. It is a single-page HTML/JavaScript app that runs analytic signal equations on a procedural 3D head phantom. This report has not evaluated its features, but it is the natural specification for the MVP.

**Realism depends more on phantoms than on physics.** The procedural head should give way to voxel maps of T1, T2, proton density and fat. Dryad's SAFE 3D MRF set is the closest ready-made fit, but its licence needs confirming. It holds 1 mm T1, T2 and PD maps from **20 healthy adults at 3T**, with coil and B0/B1 maps **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Dryad](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.brv15dvpj)). OpenNeuro ds005851 adds R1, R2* and PD maps from 138 adults but lacks T2 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([OHBM 2025](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/325811/1/OHBM2025_abstract_AgingData_submission.pdf)). IXI images need segmentation plus literature tissue values, for example from Stanisz et al. 2005 or the IT'IS database **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Stanisz](https://www.scilit.net/publications/99103192bf65b0f1130777d04b94c22a); [IT'IS](https://itis.swiss/assets/Downloads/TissueDb/V5-0/References_T1-T2_Properties_database_V5.0.pdf)). The open datasets for spine, knee and shoulder, the regions radiographers scan most, are restricted. The cleanest long-term route is a small quantitative library from **10–20 consenting volunteers** at an Indian partner hospital, collected under ethics approval and DPDP-compliant consent, which you would then own outright. Use copyleft simulators as references only. Columbia's Virtual Scanner (AGPL) and SpinSight (GPL) would force a closed SaaS to disclose its source **[UNVERIFIED]** ([JOSS](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.01637.pdf); [ISMRM 2025](https://archive.ismrm.org/2025/1968.html)).

**The stack must run on mid-range Android phones.** Android holds **93.89% of India's mobile market** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/india)). WebGPU now ships in major browsers, including Chrome on Android 12+ **[UNVERIFIED]** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers)), but the engine should still choose at runtime between WebGPU, WebGL2 and WASM back ends. NiiVue (BSD-2) or Cornerstone3D (MIT) can handle viewing **[UNVERIFIED]** ([NiiVue](https://pypi.org/project/jupyterlab-niivue/); [Cornerstone3D](https://www.cornerstonejs.org/docs/getting-started/overview)), with a custom React console on top. Supabase Pro costs **US$25 a month for 100,000 monthly active users**, and row-level security keeps colleges' data apart **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Cloudflare R2 serves phantom packs with **no egress fees** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([R2 calculator](https://r2-calculator.cloudflare.com/)). Together, that puts estimated hosting at **US$50–150 a month for 1,000–20,000 students**, about ₹4,000–13,000 at roughly ₹88 to the dollar **[ESTIMATE]**. Browser code can always be copied, so the moat is the licensed content, the institution features and the updates, backed by signed URLs and per-seat limits.

**Build what educators say existing simulators lack.** The MVP needs patient registration that feeds weight into SAR, coil selection, protocol trees for each body region, three-plane localiser planning with saturation bands, the full parameter set, live readouts of scan time, relative SNR, voxel size and SAR, the core artefacts, and an instructor task builder that auto-scores against target parameters. The differentiators lie in gaps the literature flags. A 2024 scoping review found weak skills in complex MR safety screening **[UNVERIFIED]** ([scoping review](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edselp,S1078817423001153)), and a serious game for safety training raised awareness ([Asia Pacific Scholar](https://doaj.org/article/a53c6e95989c4f7baaa1234ec94deca9)). Even a physical tabletop scanner "could not fully simulate" patient preparation and positioning ([MDPI](https://mdpi.com/2227-7102/15/7/930)). That argues for branching safety-screening patients, positioning and contrast-workflow scenarios. Colleges will also want LTI 1.3 grade return to Moodle **[UNVERIFIED]** ([LTIaaS](https://docs.ltiaas.com/learn/does-moodle-support-lti-1-3)) and an offline mode for labs with poor connectivity.

By engineering judgement, an MVP takes about **1,000–1,600 hours**: six to nine months for one strong developer, or three to five months for two or three developers with you as domain lead. The full product takes **4,000–7,000 hours** **[ESTIMATE]**. At Indian freelance rates of US$20–52 an hour **[UNVERIFIED]** ([SecondTalent](https://www.secondtalent.com/cost-to-hire/india/)), the MVP costs roughly ₹17–50 lakh, and maintenance runs at 15–25% of build effort each year **[ESTIMATE]**. For a founder who does not code, the workable path treats the prototype as the specification and uses AI-assisted development for the MVP, under milestone contracts that keep the repository and IP in your name. Add a professional code and security review before taking payments or storing student data, and 20–40 hours of an MR physicist's time to check the equations **[ESTIMATE]**.

**Validation can become your strongest sales asset with colleges.** The only randomised classroom study found was a one-day trial with 60 students, published in *Sensors* in 2021. The simulator group learned significantly more than the group taught traditionally **[UNVERIFIED]** ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/21/18/6011)). A 2021 *Radiography* review concluded that the benefits of computer-based simulation "have not been fully assessed" **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Bradford Scholars](https://bradscholars.brad.ac.uk/entities/publication/5b0e2c8d-c6a8-4d4b-9abb-0d4d48f3e695)). No Indian study exists. A multi-college Kerala study would combine four checks: physics validated against KomaMRI and phantom scans, blinded real-versus-simulated ratings by experienced radiographers, System Usability Scale scores, and a pre/post-test with a delayed practical assessment. Published in *Radiography* or the *Indian Journal of Radiology & Imaging*, it would supply the evidence that Corsmed's vendor case studies do not.

## Conclusion

The question changed six days before this report. Corsmed's free tier made the simulator itself a commodity, so building one is no longer the opportunity. The opportunity is what Mallu Radiographer holds and Corsmed, ScanFlo and MRI Education do not: a trusted Malayalam channel to radiographers at every step from Kerala student to MRI job to Gulf or UK registration. The strongest framing makes the console the hands-on layer of that career product, able to sit alongside free tools rather than fight them. The economics follow. An outsourced, full-price build cannot be repaid from a Kerala market of under ₹1 crore a year. A founder-led build that starts from the existing prototype, runs in the browser and costs about a lakh a year to host can cover its running costs comfortably with a few hundred paying users and a handful of college licences **[ESTIMATE]**.

Before committing serious money, run four cheap checks: name the Kerala rival, search and file the trade mark, clear the venture with your employer, and get a patent attorney's view on Corsmed's web slice-selection claims. After that, the most defensible investment is not more features but India's first published outcome study of MRI simulation. In a field where incumbents rely on case studies, that evidence would give you an edge with colleges that is hard for any competitor to copy.
