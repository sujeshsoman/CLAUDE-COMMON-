# Start in Manchester, expand into Kerala later

> **General information, not legal or tax advice.** This report explains the rules in plain English so you can ask your advisers better questions. Before you register a company, take on a co-founder or investor, move country or change how you pay yourself, confirm the position with a UK chartered accountant or chartered tax adviser and with a Kerala chartered accountant (CA) or company secretary (CS). Some points also need an immigration adviser or an employment lawyer, and the checklist near the end says which.

**Register a UK private limited company in Manchester first. Sell every individual subscription through a "merchant of record" that handles foreign sales tax for you, and host on a global network rather than on servers in Kerala. Use Kerala as your sales and development base, not your legal home, until a Kerala company can pay for itself.** While you live and pay tax in Manchester, a UK company keeps your company tax and your personal tax in one country. It costs **£100** to register and **£50** a year to keep registered ([Companies House fees](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-fees/companies-house-fees)). It also avoids the trap of running an Indian company from Manchester, which could leave that company tax-resident in both countries with no treaty relief unless the two tax authorities agree ([UK–India treaty text](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/india-tax-treaties/2020-uk-india-synthesised-text-of-the-multilateral-instrument-and-the-1993-double-taxation-convention-in-force)). Your belief about Kerala is half right. People, desks and accountants there cost a fraction of UK prices, and Kerala Startup Mission (KSUM) offers grants of **₹3–15 lakh** per stage and a **₹15 lakh** loan that a UK company cannot get ([KSUM](https://startupmission.kerala.gov.in/schemes/early-stage-funding)). The data-protection, consumer and sales-tax rules that bite hardest follow your customers, not your company, so a Kerala base does not shrink them. Kerala servers would be slower and no cheaper: the simulator is a 64 KB file that computes images on the student's phone, and a global network plus managed logins costs about **US$30–45 a month for up to 50,000 users** **[ESTIMATE]**. Give the simulator its own product name and website, branded "by Mallu Radiographer", and keep malluradiographer.com as the shop window showing the "MRI simulator" and "CT simulator" choices. Sell first to Malayali radiographers heading to, or newly arrived in, the NHS, at **£49–149 a pack** **[ESTIMATE]**, then to NHS CT/MRI departments and Kerala colleges. Build the CT trainer, because CT is the bigger modality in all three of your markets, but make it the second product, starting around month 4: ScanFlo already sells a browser CT simulator in India from **₹899 a month** ([ScanFlo](https://scanflo.io/pricing)). ## A UK company suits a founder who lives and pays tax in Manchester

Your Indefinite Leave to Remain lets you "live, work and study here for as long as you like" ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/indefinite-leave-to-remain)), so immigration law does not stop you being a sole trader, a company director or a shareholder. A private limited company costs **£100** (₹12,639) to register online and is usually registered within 24 hours ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/register-your-company)). The yearly confirmation statement then costs **£50** (₹6,320) ([Companies House fees](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-fees/companies-house-fees)), and since **18 November 2025** every director of a new company must verify their identity with Companies House and give a personal code at registration ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/when-you-need-to-verify-your-identity-for-companies-house)). Add the data-protection fee of **£52** a year, or £47 by direct debit ([SI 2025/63](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/63/made)), an accountant at roughly **£1,200–3,000** a year **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Sleek](https://sleek.com/uk/resources/how-much-does-an-accountant-cost/); [Accounts OS](https://accounts-os.com/blog/limited-company-accountant-cost-uk)), and insurance. A small UK company then runs at about **£1,600–5,300 a year** (₹2.0–6.7 lakh) before payment fees and hosting **[ESTIMATE]**. The yearly paperwork is light: accounts, a Corporation Tax return, the confirmation statement, and payroll once you pay yourself a salary. Late accounts cost between £150 and £1,500, depending on how late they are ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/annual-accounts/penalties-for-late-filing)). Two changes arrive in April 2028. Accounts must be filed through commercial software, which most accountants already use, and a planned rule forcing small companies to publish their profit-and-loss account has been paused ([Companies House](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/companies-house-update-to-accounts-filing-from-april-2028)).

**Choose the company for protection and credibility, not to save tax.** Corporation Tax is **19%** on profits up to £50,000 and 25% above £250,000 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/corporation-tax-rates)). In 2026-27, dividends above a £500 allowance are taxed at **10.75%** at the basic rate and **35.75%** at the higher rate ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-dividends)). While your NHS salary uses up your tax-free allowance and puts you in the higher-rate band, taking £40,000 of company profit out as dividends costs about **47.5%** in total tax, against about **44.1%** for the same profit earned as a sole trader **[ESTIMATE]**. Once you go full-time and fall to the basic rate, the gap narrows to about **27.7% against 26%** **[ESTIMATE]**. What the company buys you instead is limited liability, which protects your personal assets if the business is sued or runs up debts. It also gives you a supplier name that reassures NHS trusts and colleges, and the freedom to leave profit inside the company at 19% until your own income falls. It opens up SEIS, under which outside investors get 50% income-tax relief and the company can raise up to £250,000 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/venture-capital-schemes-tax-relief-for-investors); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/venture-capital-schemes-apply-to-use-the-seed-enterprise-investment-scheme)). That relief cannot go to you personally if you hold more than 30% of the shares, because it is meant for outside investors who pay UK tax (same source). Finally, a company is outside Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, the quarterly digital reporting that from April 2028 will catch sole traders with more than £20,000 of turnover in 2026-27 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-youre-eligible-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)). Two details matter while you are still employed. A director's salary mostly adds tax, because your NHS job already uses your personal allowance **[ESTIMATE]**. And a company whose only employee is its single director cannot claim the £10,500 Employment Allowance, which cuts the employer's National Insurance bill ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/claim-employment-allowance/eligibility)).

Your personal tax points the same way. As a long-term UK resident you pay UK tax on your worldwide income in the year it arises. The new four-year regime for foreign income only helps people arriving after ten years abroad, so it does not apply to you ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-can-claim-the-4-year-foreign-income-and-gains-regime)). If you owned an Indian company and paid yourself dividends, India could withhold up to **10%** under the UK–India treaty. The UK would then tax the same dividend again, giving credit only for that withholding ([UK–India treaty](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/india-tax-treaties/2020-uk-india-synthesised-text-of-the-multilateral-instrument-and-the-1993-double-taxation-convention-in-force)). The Indian company's own corporate tax would not be credited to you as an individual. That tax is about **25.17%** under the optional 22% regime **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/domestic-company-tax-rate/)). The same profit would therefore be taxed at two levels in two countries **[ESTIMATE]**.

**The biggest hidden risk is running an Indian company from Manchester.** A company registered in India is always tax-resident in India. If you take its real decisions in Manchester, the UK can treat it as UK-resident as well, under the "central management and control" test from UK case law; HMRC's guidance on that test was not read in this research. The treaty also counts a "place of management" as a permanent establishment (PE), meaning a taxable presence in the other country. Since the multilateral instrument (MLI) amended the treaty, a company resident in both countries gets **no treaty relief at all** unless the two tax authorities agree where it belongs ([UK–India treaty](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/india-tax-treaties/2020-uk-india-synthesised-text-of-the-multilateral-instrument-and-the-1993-double-taxation-convention-in-force)).

POEM, the "place of effective management" test you may have heard of, is India's version, and it works in the opposite direction: it catches a UK company that is run day to day by someone in Kerala. Separately, a company whose people provide services in India for more than **90 days** in a year can create a PE there (same source). Owning both a UK and an Indian company adds a smaller trap. Companies under the same control count as "associated", which halves the UK company's 19% band to the first £25,000 of profit ([CTA 2010 s18E](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/4/section/18E); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/corporation-tax-marginal-relief)). There are two clean set-ups. For now, that is one UK company, with your Kerala partner as a contractor whose role is written down. Later, if needed, it is a UK company with a properly run Indian subsidiary that charges the same prices an unrelated firm would ("arm's-length" prices).

Write down your trusted person's role before they sell anything. India's GST rules say that a person in India who represents an overseas seller of online services "for any purpose" must register for and pay the Indian GST on that seller's behalf ([CBIC OIDAR flyer](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/pdf/e-version-gst-fliers/OIDAR_online%20version_online%20version_20_July_2017.pdf)). Two steps limit that exposure: keep all individual sales on a merchant of record (next section), and give your partner a written contract as a paid sales-and-support contractor. One question remains open. A contractor who regularly signs college deals might create a PE for your UK company. The treaty's clauses on agents were not reviewed here, so that is a question for a cross-border tax adviser.

### Moving to Kerala would flip the answer

Everything above assumes you keep living in Manchester. If going full-time means moving home, three things change. Your ILR lapses after **more than two continuous years** outside the UK, and coming back then needs a Returning Resident visa costing **£726** (₹91,800) plus evidence of "strong ties" ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/returning-resident-visa)). Your UK tax residence ends once you spend little time here: under the statutory residence test, fewer than 16 days in a tax year usually makes you non-resident ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/tax-foreign-income/residence)). And a UK company that you then run from Kerala would carry the Indian residence and PE risk described above, so an Indian private limited company becomes the natural home. Once you spend 182 days a year in India, you could be its resident director yourself **[UNVERIFIED]** ([SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/resident-director-vs-designated-partner-residency-rules)). The UK Ltd could be closed for **£13** online ([Companies House fees](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-fees/companies-house-fees)) or kept as a UK sales arm. If a move is more than a year away, starting with the cheap UK company still makes sense. Either way, decide where you will live for the next two to three years before you register anything, because the company should follow the founder.

The pattern is simple. Kerala wins wherever the cost is people's time, and loses wherever the cost is rules. Indian late fees look small in pounds, at ₹100 a day for each missing form **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/annual-compliance-cost-for-private-limited-company-in-india/)), but the real penalties are not money. A missed director KYC deactivates that director's identification number, which blocks the company's filings until it is fixed **[UNVERIFIED]** ([IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/director-kyc-dir3-annual-filing)). Three years of missed filings disqualify the directors **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/annual-compliance-cost-for-private-limited-company-in-india/)), and a company flagged "Active Non-compliant" cannot get the KSUM seed loan ([KSUM Seed Fund](https://startupmission.kerala.gov.in/schemes/seed-fund)). Export sales escape GST only if all five legal export conditions are met, including payment in foreign currency backed by bank evidence, and missing any one makes the sale taxable ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/conditions-for-export-of-services-under-gst/)).

There is also a question of control specific to you. An Indian company needs a resident director, and Razorpay's international-payments set-up requires a live video KYC with an Aadhaar and a PAN card ([Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/international-payments.md)). While you are in Manchester, your trusted person would hold the bank mandate, the payment account and the company's legal signature. That can work well, but only with a shareholders' agreement that sets out who owns what, who decides what, and what happens if either of you leaves.

Kerala's real advantages arrive once the business has a product and customers there. KSUM's funding ladder is open and generous. The Idea Grant pays up to **₹3 lakh**, but only to "innovators based out of Kerala" ([KSUM Idea Grant](https://startupmission.kerala.gov.in/schemes/idea-grant)). The Productisation Grant pays up to **₹7 lakh**, or just the remaining ₹4 lakh if you already had the Idea Grant, and you must put in 20% of your own money ("margin money") at its second instalment ([KSUM Productisation Grant](https://startupmission.kerala.gov.in/schemes/productisation-grant)). The Market Acceleration Grant pays up to **₹10 lakh**. The seed loan lends up to **₹15 lakh** at 6% simple interest, with no repayments for the first 12 months ([KSUM](https://startupmission.kerala.gov.in/schemes/early-stage-funding)). It is lent against purchase orders, though, and is "not generally given for service startups" ([KSUM Seed Fund](https://startupmission.kerala.gov.in/schemes/seed-fund)). If every grant application succeeded, that would be about **₹17 lakh (£13,450)**, plus the loan **[ESTIMATE]**. Each scheme needs a Kerala-registered private limited company or LLP. A start-up recognised by the central government's DPIIT can also claim a three-year income-tax holiday within its first ten years ([Startup India](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html)). KSUM even runs a programme for non-resident Indians, Startup Infinity, although its benefits page could not be read ([KSUM](https://startupmission.kerala.gov.in/schemes/business)). These are reasons to open a Kerala company in year 2, not to make it your first legal home.

## One merchant of record replaces four foreign tax registrations

Whichever country you choose, selling online to individuals abroad creates sales-tax duties from the very first sale. A UK company charges UK VAT only to UK consumers, and only once its UK taxable sales pass **£90,000** a year (₹1.14 crore), because sales to consumers outside the UK are not liable to UK VAT ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-vat-rules-if-you-supply-digital-services-to-private-consumers)). India, however, makes an overseas seller of online services pay Indian GST itself, through a simplified registration on Form GST REG-10 ([CBIC](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/pdf/e-version-gst-fliers/OIDAR_online%20version_online%20version_20_July_2017.pdf)). India calls these "OIDAR" services, and a 2023 change brought online education clearly inside the definition. There is reportedly no threshold, returns are monthly, and the rate is 18% **[UNVERIFIED]** ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/indias-oidar-taxation-shift-key-changes-effective-october-1-2023-29757.html); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/oidar-gst-registration)). The UAE (5%) and Saudi Arabia (15%) reportedly require registration from the first consumer sale **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Avalara UAE](https://www.avalara.com/vatlive/en/country-guides/africa-and-middle-east/united-arab-emirates/uae-vat-on-digital-and-electronic-services.html); [Avalara KSA](https://www.avalara.com/vatlive/en/country-guides/africa-and-middle-east/saudi-arabia/saudi-arabia-vat-on-digital-and-e-services.html)), and the EU scheme for sellers based outside the EU applies from the first euro **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Taxually](https://support.taxually.com/support/solutions/articles/80001155457)). An Indian company faces the mirror image: it must register for UK VAT before its first sale to a UK consumer ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration/when-to-register)).

A **merchant of record** solves this. It is a payment company that legally resells your product, so it charges and files the VAT and GST in each country, not you. **Paddle** charges **5% + 50¢** per sale with no monthly fee, and says it collects the correct tax, tracks filing deadlines and submits returns ([Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)). It now accepts UPI for one-off purchases and UPI AutoPay for subscriptions, processed in rupees ([Paddle blog](https://www.paddle.com/blog/india-payment-methods-offering-right-ways-to-pay)), with UPI renewals reportedly capped at ₹15,000 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Paddle docs](https://developer.paddle.com/concepts/payment-methods/upi)). Paddle is built for software companies and refuses to sell courses the seller did not create ([Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/what-am-i-not-allowed-to-sell-on-paddle)). A simulator you built yourself fits, but ask Paddle to confirm before you build checkout around it. Stripe's equivalent, Managed Payments, adds **3.5%** on top of normal Stripe fees ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/gb/managed-payments)).

That is ScanFlo's model of one-off packs that never renew automatically ([ScanFlo](https://scanflo.io/pricing)). Institutions are simpler because they are businesses. NHS trusts and UK universities pay by invoice or purchase order. Indian colleges registered for GST account for the tax themselves under "reverse charge" ([CBIC](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/pdf/e-version-gst-fliers/OIDAR_online%20version_online%20version_20_July_2017.pdf)). Many private colleges may not be registered, though, and sales to them could count as consumer sales that need your own Indian GST registration **[ESTIMATE]**. Indian payers may also withhold tax on licence fees if they treat them as royalties. The treaty caps that at 15%, and the UK company can offset it against its UK Corporation Tax ([UK–India treaty](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/india-tax-treaties/2020-uk-india-synthesised-text-of-the-multilateral-instrument-and-the-1993-double-taxation-convention-in-force)). A handful of Kerala college deals can be invoiced from the UK. Dozens of them would be the clearest signal to open a Kerala company.

If you want to collect rupees directly, there are two possible routes. Stripe UK lists UPI for UK businesses at 2%, plus 1.5% for international payments, plus 2% for currency conversion ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/gb/payment-method/upi)). Razorpay is less clear: one of its help pages offers foreign businesses Indian payment methods on request ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/international-payments.md)), but another mentions only American businesses ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/international-payments/)), so ask Razorpay directly. Either way, collecting rupees yourself brings back the Indian GST registration that a merchant of record would have handled.

## Data rules follow the customer, so the servers can sit anywhere

**Data protection is not a reason to choose either country, because both countries' rules follow your customers.** UK GDPR applies to everything a UK company does. It also applies to a foreign company offering services to people in the UK ([UK GDPR Art 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/3)), and that foreign company must appoint a UK representative in writing unless its UK data handling is only occasional ([UK GDPR Art 27](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/27)). A representative costs roughly US$326–551, £490 or €875 a year **[UNVERIFIED]** ([AWS Marketplace](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-7aidli3kkc2ya); [euverify](https://euverify.com/appoint-your-gdpr-representative-in-the-eu-uk/); [IT Governance](https://www.itgovernance.eu/shop/product/gdpr-uk-representative)). India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act likewise covers any company, anywhere, that offers services to people in India ([DPDP Act s.3](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)). Whichever company you form, you will work under both laws.

On the UK side, most changes from the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 started on **5 February 2026** ([SI 2026/82](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/82/made)). For a small edtech business they mostly reduce friction. Your own analytics, used only to improve the service, no longer need a consent pop-up, provided you explain them and give users a free, simple way to object ([DUAA Sch 12](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/schedule/12)). Three changes add duties. From 19 June 2026 you must run a process for data-protection complaints ([SI 2026/82](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/82/made)). Services likely to be used by children must be designed with their protection in mind ([DUAA s.81](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/81)). And fines for breaking the marketing and cookie rules now reach £17.5 million or 4% of turnover **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/zh-hans/insights/publications/2025/06/the-data-use-and-access-act-pecr-reform-rules-relating-to-electronic-marketing-and-cookies-in-the-uk)). The regulator, formerly the ICO, became the Information Commission on 30 September 2026 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-use-and-access-act-2025-plans-for-commencement)).

India is stricter on the one issue that matters most for students. Its DPDP Rules were published on 13 November 2025, and the main duties start **18 months later, around 13 May 2027**: notice, consent, security, breach reporting, children and penalties ([MeitY commencement notice](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf); [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)). A "child" is anyone under **18**, which includes many first-year radiography students. You need verifiable parental consent, and you may not track, behaviourally monitor or target advertising at children ([DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)). Maximum penalties reach **₹250 crore** for weak security and ₹200 crore for breaking the rules on children (same source). In early 2026 the ministry consulted on cutting the 18 months to 12, which would mean **13 November 2026**, five days after this report; the researchers found no notice adopting that change **[UNVERIFIED]** ([S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/)). Separately, India's cyber-security agency, CERT-In, requires incidents to be reported within **6 hours** and logs kept for **180 days** "within the Indian jurisdiction", and its FAQ extends this to foreign companies serving Indian users **[UNVERIFIED]** ([CERT-In Directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf); [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/Decoding-the-new-CERT-In-Directions:-Government-releases-the-much-awaited-FAQs)).

Three design choices remove most of the risk under both laws. First, **make direct sign-up 18+ only**, with an age confirmation. Second, **bring under-18 students in only through college licences**. The DPDP Rules exempt an "educational institution" from the parental-consent and tracking limits for its own educational activities ([DPDP Rules, Fourth Schedule](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)), so the college becomes responsible for its students' data and you process it for the college under contract. Third, **keep the free demo scan open without sign-up**. India's consumer regulator reportedly fined PhysicsWallah ₹5 lakh in June 2026, partly for demanding personal details before users could open "free" content **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/1799382/dark-patterns-in-ed-tech-ccpa-imposes-penalty-on-physicswallah)). UK buyers need two more things. Checkout needs a box in which they agree to start straight away and accept that they lose their 14-day right to cancel; without it they can cancel and pay nothing ([CCR reg 37](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/37)). And the new subscription rules expected in **spring 2027** will require renewal reminders and easy online cancellation ([DBT](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/consultation-on-the-implementation-of-the-new-subscription-contracts-regime/outcome/government-response-to-consultation-on-the-implementation-of-the-new-subscription-contracts-regime-web-accessible-version)). The researchers put the paperwork both bases share at 4–8 working days of your time **[ESTIMATE]**. They judge a UK base only slightly less work, because it avoids the UK representative and makes CERT-In's reach less certain. The difference is a few hundred pounds **[ESTIMATE]**.

### Servers in Kerala would be slower, not cheaper

The prototype explains why location hardly matters. `mri-console/index.html` is **206,173 bytes, or 65,576 bytes compressed**, and loads nothing from other websites (measured on the file in this repository). A student downloads about 64 KB once, and the phone computes every image. Only logging in, checking that the user has paid and downloading paid modules need a server, perhaps two to five requests a session **[ESTIMATE]**. So even the roughly **125 ms** round trip between Mumbai and London **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Anurag Bhatia](https://anuragbhatia.com/post/2025/06/india-europe-high-latency/)) adds well under a second to a session.

No Indian rule forces an edtech business to store data in India. The DPDP Act restricts transfers only to countries the government names, and in-country storage applies only to designated "significant" data handlers ([DPDP Act s.16](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf); [DPDP Rules r.13](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)). RBI's rule on storing payment data binds payment companies, not shops ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=130)). The researchers found no Kerala-based host with its own data centre; the nearest big-cloud regions are Mumbai and Hyderabad ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/global-infrastructure/regions_az/)). A cheap Indian host such as Hostinger lists ₹599–1,899 a month on long prepaid terms, with higher renewal prices **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Zoutons](https://zoutons.com/news/hostinger-cloud-hosting-price-india-2026)). But that is a single server you would have to update, back up and defend yourself.

Serve the app itself from a global content-delivery network (CDN), which keeps copies on servers inside India. AWS CloudFront's flat-rate plans cost **US$0** a month (1 million requests, 100 GB) or **US$15** a month (10 million requests, 50 TB), with a firewall, attack protection and security certificate included ([AWS CloudFront](https://aws.amazon.com/cloudfront/pricing/)). Cloudflare Pages is reportedly free with no traffic limit **[UNVERIFIED]** ([makerkit](https://makerkit.dev/pricing-calculator/cloudflare)), but one developer found its free plan serving Indian users from Europe **[UNVERIFIED]** ([punits.dev](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://punits.dev/blog/cloudflare-latency-india)), so test from a Jio or Airtel phone in Kerala before you choose. Put logins and the database on Supabase, at about **US$25 a month for up to 100,000 monthly users** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([makerkit](https://makerkit.dev/blog/saas/supabase-pricing)), in its London region **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Supabase docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions)). A UK company serving UK customers first then never sends their data abroad. Keep a 180-day log archive in AWS Mumbai to satisfy CERT-In cheaply. Before your Kerala partner gets admin access, sign the UK's international data transfer agreement (IDTA) with them, because India has no UK "adequacy" decision ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/all?title=Data%20Protection%20%28Adequacy%29); [UK GDPR Art 46](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/46)).

## Give the simulator its own name, with Mallu Radiographer as the front door

A Mallu Radiographer page offering an "MRI simulator" and a "CT simulator" is the right shop window. The simulator itself, though, should live on its own product domain, presented as "<ProductName> by Mallu Radiographer". Keep malluradiographer.com for videos, blog posts and Malayalam content, plus two landing pages, /mri-simulator and /ct-simulator. Each landing page gets a "Try a free scan" button that opens the app on the product domain, where login and checkout also live. If you cannot settle on a name quickly, app.malluradiographer.com is a fine stop-gap. A subdomain like that needs only one DNS setting, while a path such as /simulators would need the app and your existing site on the same platform.

Search ranking does not decide this. Google's John Mueller has said Google handles subdomains and subfolders equally well **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-treats-subdomains-subdirectories-john-mueller-says/254687/)), and Ahrefs reports that Google needs only days to learn a new subdomain **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/subdomain-vs-subfolder)). Your search traffic comes mainly from YouTube and Instagram, and the landing pages that capture it stay on malluradiographer.com either way.

Three other things do decide it. The first is **buyers and their web filters**. Dictionaries tag "Mallu" as slang that is "sometimes derogatory" **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Kaikki/Wiktionary](https://kaikki.org/dictionary/English/meaning/M/Ma/Mallu.html)), and a plain web search for the word during this research returned adult-themed pages alongside the dictionary entries. NHS and Gulf procurement teams will see the name on invoices and in their web-filter allow-lists. A neutral product name avoids an awkward conversation, and Malayali audiences still recognise "by Mallu Radiographer". The second is **what competitors do**. ScanFlo sells a product family (MRI, CT, PET-CT, Anatomy, RadPath) under its own brand, with separate pages for universities, hospitals and students ([ScanFlo](https://scanflo.io/mri)), and Corsmed is also a stand-alone brand ([Corsmed](https://www.corsmed.com/)). MRI Education keeps its simulator inside its course brand, but there the £800 course is the product ([MRI Education](https://www.mrieducation.com/)). The third is **a possible future sale**. A product with its own domain, trade mark and customer list can be sold or licensed; a product inside your personal creator brand cannot be separated from you.

Before you buy the domain, run a trade-mark clearance search and file the new name in classes 9, 41 and 42 (software, education and online services). Filing online in India costs ₹4,500 a class **[UNVERIFIED]** ([IP India](https://www.ipindia.gov.in/pages/trade-marks/learn/forms-and-official-fees)); UK fees were not researched. Test the name with five NHS or Gulf colleagues.

## CT is the bigger modality, but ScanFlo got there first

**Build a CT trainer, but as the second product and a better one, not on the assumption that the field is empty.** CT outnumbers MRI on every measure the researchers found. England performed about **0.71 million CT scans against 0.41 million MRI** in March 2026 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/statistics/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/07/Statistical-Release-23rd-July-2026.pdf)), or about 1.7 CT scans per MRI **[ESTIMATE]**. India's AERB portal listed **8,202 CT scanners** in August 2023, a figure ScanFlo's own blog cites from the Competition Commission's 2024 study ([ScanFlo blog](https://scanflo.io/blog/ct-vs-mri-which-modality-to-specialise-in)), against an estimated fewer than 5,000 MRI scanners **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Hyperfine](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240528157535/en)). One Kerala medical college ran **85 CT scans a day against 25 MRI** **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2022/04/26/alappuzha-medical-college-fraud.html)). Gulf exams test CT directly. Saudi Arabia's radiology-technology promotion exam gives advanced CT physics 30% of the marks, and a separate CT clinical promotion exam was published in June 2026 **[UNVERIFIED]** ([SCFHS](https://www.scfhs.org.sa/sites/default/files/2025-03/Radiology%20Technology%20Residency%20Promotion%20Exam%20Blueprint%2024-27%20-%20R1%20and%20R2.pdf); [SCFHS CT R3](https://scfhs.org.sa/sites/default/files/2026-06/CT%20Clinical%20Promotion%20Exam%20Blueprint%20R3.pdf)). Dubai employers advertise "CT and MRI" technologist posts at AED 10,000–12,000 a month **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Careerjet](https://www.careerjet.ae/jobad/ae669b10da3a58d2b3a37f261f129bf4d6)).

An early pass of this round's research assumed that browser CT with rupee pricing was open ground. It is not. **ScanFlo CT** already runs in the browser. It offers scout planning, helical and axial modes, kV and mAs with live effects on image quality and dose, pitch and collimation, CTDIvol and DLP on every scan, kernels, windows, MPR and 3D, plus contrast-timing guides ([ScanFlo CT](https://scanflo.io/ct)). It costs **₹899 a month or ₹8,999 a year**, or US$39 or US$399 outside India, and is sold as a separate plan from MRI ([ScanFlo pricing](https://scanflo.io/pricing)). The rest of the field is further from your market. Shaderware's TomoVR is quote-only software used at "well over 100" sites **[UNVERIFIED]** ([HealthySimulation](https://www.healthysimulation.com/vendors/shaderware/)). ScanLabCT comes inside US$1,700–2,500 American course bundles **[UNVERIFIED]** ([ScanLab](https://scanlabmr.com/ct-individual-cohorts/)), and Siemens' cloud simulator serves only Siemens customers **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Siemens Healthineers](https://www.siemens-healthineers.com/services/customer-services/upskill-services/equipment-and-clinical-education/individual-education-and-practice/smartsimulator)).

The openings lie in what ScanFlo's CT page does not mention, and they happen to be the mistakes radiographers make on real patients. ScanFlo asks for a laptop or desktop ([ScanFlo CT](https://scanflo.io/ct)), while your MRI prototype already runs on an emulated mid-range phone. Its page also says nothing about artefacts, automatic exposure control (AEC) or off-centring. Off-centring is the everyday error in which a patient lying away from the scanner's centre changes the tube current and the dose. NHS-bound learners would also get a strong lesson from a dose readout checked against the UK's national reference levels, such as **47 mGy CTDIvol and 790 mGy·cm DLP** for an acute-stroke head, or 9.1 mGy and 310 mGy·cm for a CT pulmonary angiogram ([GOV.UK NDRLs](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/diagnostic-radiology-national-diagnostic-reference-levels-ndrls/ndrl)). India's installed CT base is mostly GE (about 45%) and Siemens (about 31%) **[UNVERIFIED]** ([CCI](https://www.cci.gov.in/public/images/marketstudie/en/market-study-of-diagnostic-medical-imaging-equipment-industry-in-india1724145632.pdf)), and students transfer skills better when the simulator resembles their placement scanner **[UNVERIFIED]** ([Monash](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/transferability-of-learning-from-computed-tomography-simulation-i/)). So offer optional "GE-style" and "Siemens-style" terms (noise index or reference mAs, scout or topogram) without copying either vendor's screens. Finally, sell MRI and CT together for less than the **₹1,898 a month** that two ScanFlo student plans cost **[ESTIMATE]**.

CT is easier to build than MRI in physics terms. The image is essentially a map of tissue densities plus noise, blur, windowing and artefacts, and the MRI prototype's 3D phantom, viewer and console shell can be reused (the researchers' assessment; no outside source). If CT needs about the same freelance hours as the business features in the plan below, it costs roughly ₹60,000–6,25,000 (£475–4,945) **[ESTIMATE]**, plus a CT physicist's check of the dose tables. Two cautions apply. **Call it a "CT scanning trainer"**, because in India "CT simulator" usually means radiotherapy planning hardware, as in a 2026 Tata Memorial tender **[UNVERIFIED]** ([TMC tender](https://docs.infralens.in/nit/2026_TMC_908208_1/Tendernotice_1.pdf)). And **never claim it counts toward AERB or IR(ME)R competence**. AERB's route to becoming a CT radiation safety officer, for instance, requires three years of CT experience **[UNVERIFIED]** ([AERB](https://aerb.gov.in/images/PDF/DiagnosticRadiology/REGULATORY-REQUIREMENTS-FOR-DIAGNOSTIC-RADIOLOGY-PRACTICE.pdf)), and nothing found shows any regulator accepting simulator hours.

Make the free CT demo a head scan with a deliberately off-centred patient, so the learner sees the dose jump. Start the build in month 4, once the MRI paywall and accounts work, so that CT can reuse them. If your audience data shows mostly Kerala- or Gulf-based followers, bring CT forward, because CT is the skill they are hired for.

## Conclusion

"UK or Kerala?" turns out to be two questions with different answers. Where the company is registered should follow where you live and take decisions, because that is what tax authorities look at. Where the work is done and where customers are found can be Kerala from day one. Kerala's low costs are real, but they are low costs for people, and a UK company can buy that by paying Kerala contractors without taking on an Indian audit, FEMA filings and the risk of being tax-resident in two countries. The rules you hoped to escape — data protection, consumer law and sales tax — were never set by your address. They are set by your customers' addresses. A merchant of record turns the sales-tax part into a 5% fee, and a few design choices handle most of the rest: 18+ sign-up, under-18s only through colleges, and no personal data demanded before the free scan.

The simulator market has also moved since the first report. Basic MRI practice is free at Corsmed and cheap at ScanFlo, and CT is no longer an empty field. What remains scarce is what you already have: NHS credibility, a Malayali audience on the path from Kerala to the NHS, and a clinician's eye for the mistakes that hurt real patients, such as off-centring, creeping dose and artefacts. Build the paid product around those mistakes. Then watch the two dates that could change this plan: the day you decide whether to move home, and the day India's data-protection duties actually start.
